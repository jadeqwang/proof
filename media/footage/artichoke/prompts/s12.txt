SUBJECT. A mature globe artichoke plant (Cynara cardunculus var. scolymus) at the moment its first flower bud is ready, drawn whole. A broad, arching rosette of very large, deeply pinnately lobed leaves, silvery grey-green above with paler woolly whitish undersides, the lobes jagged and softly spined, thick pale midribs; the leaves arch outward and droop gracefully at their tips, the lowest ones resting near the earth on both sides, the whole rosette spanning only about 44% of the sheet's width. From the centre rises one stout, upright, ribbed, grey-green flowering stem with a few small clasping leaves, and at its top, at about 24% down from the top of the sheet (the top of the bud no higher than 20% down), one large closed artichoke bud: a firm globe of overlapping broad green scale-like bracts, each bract tipped and edged with a faint violet-purple flush, drawn with the finest line in the plate. One smaller closed side bud on a short side branch lower on the stem. The plant is perfectly frontal and symmetrical in its balance, calm and still.

COLOUR. Only a few restrained washes: soft silvery sage and grey-green for the leaves, a slightly yellower green on the young leaves at the centre and on the bud, a breath of violet on the bract tips, warm umber for the earth. The paper is left bare everywhere else.


SCALE. The plant is drawn SMALL on the sheet, with wide empty paper all round it: everything fits inside the central 46% of the sheet's width, and between 20% and 76% of the way down. Wide bare margins of paper to the left and right of the plant and above it.
