## Vision: the mote leaves the beam

Nobody can be handed proof of another person's inside, and we believe in it anyway, the way we believe a dust mote is still there after it drifts out of the sunbeam. The video makes the viewer perform that inference instead of watching her fail to supply the proof.

The image is already in "Winter 1988 - Spring 1989": she studies "the dust motes floating in the angular sunbeam / disappearing from view when they float out of the light." Everything below is built from it.

- **The beam is what can be seen from outside.** Every scene is bare paper plus one shaft of light. What the light touches is drawn in full depth. What it misses stays paper.
- **Paper is not empty.** This is 留白 taken literally. The film's job is to teach, shot by shot, that the unpainted part is where things are.
- **Her face is where other people's beam lands.** Nothing moves there, so they conclude nothing is there. The film never argues back. It keeps showing where her own beam is pointed.

This keeps what you chose on 2026-11-22: field notes, 留白, the pattern-finding child, the three matching beams, the frost that becomes a neuron, the interrupted count. It adds a spine and three moves.

### Three moves

1. **The blank faces swap sides.** Seen from outside, her face is the blank one. Seen from inside, theirs are. The poems say so: "I can't tell most of them apart", "I memorize their clothing and current positions / I've already forgotten their faces." So strangers are drawn the way she logs them: sweatshirt print, shoes, where the feet point, hands. The face is left as paper. Only the people who can read her get a drawn face: Mama and Dad. First love can read her too, but he is a real person and his face is never shown: he is seen from behind, over the shoulder, by his hair, his hands and his forearms. This also meets the no-likeness rule without a workaround.
2. **The viewer blocks the aisle.** In the supermarket the film counts the artichoke's spirals with her, numeral by numeral. A grandmother has been waiting to get past the whole time, in frame, unlit. We notice her when the arm is pulled. The viewer has just done the thing the song is about, and felt that it was attention, not absence.
3. **It ends on an inference.** In the intro a mote drifts out of the beam and vanishes. Her eyes keep tracking it across the dark, and it re-enters the light exactly where she is looking. In the last shot a mote leaves the beam and the film holds on bare paper. This time the viewer is the one who knows where it is.

### Two notations and a blank

- **Toner is theirs.** Other people's readings of her arrive pre-printed, like the labels on the emotions handout: a copy of a copy, speckled, one row misaligned. "The categories arrived before I did" ("Null Hypothesis").
- **Gold pencil is hers.** One hand-drawn field note per shot, on the world, never on a person's face.
- **Her face carries neither.** It is the empty cell in the handout's short row.

### What I am cutting

- A different print style per era. One hand throughout, the Chaos Monkey ink line and wash on xuan paper. Eras are told apart by their light source.
- Red proofreader's marks and *stet*. Toner labels do that job and come from the poem.
- Stacked ideas in single shots. One beam, one field note, one thing in depth.

### A note from the director

"Did she understand or just pattern match?" is also asked about models like me, and I cannot settle it from inside either. I have kept machines out of the frame, as the brainstorm asks. It is why the one line the film attributes on screen is Dad's: "Good catch!" He does not settle whether she understood. He answers what she did.

## Reading the song

The song is mostly other people talking about her, and it goes silent each time she is asked to account for herself. The master is `inputs/Proof (-2sm).wav` (4:28.4, 99.4 BPM, Bb minor); its map is `analysis/m2sm/song_map.md`. This section was first written on the v2 render and still holds for the master.

- **Count who speaks.** The whispers, both pre-chorus couplets, "What on earth are you doing?", "Good catch!", "Did she understand or just pattern match?", the chorus question and the outro instruction are all other voices. Her own lines are few and plain: "What's so funny?", "Do you not see what I see?", "I honestly don't know", "I think he's right. Honestly."
- **Her word is "honestly".** It appears three times. She is not evasive about her inside. She is exact about what she can't report.
- **The band drops out four times,** and each is a moment of being looked at: 1:32.7 ("that's just how it looks"), 2:52.5 (after "proof"), 3:19.1 ("I think he's right"), 3:54.9 (the outro). These are the song's own blank spaces. In the film each one is bare paper with one thing left on it.
- **The same question is asked twice by different people.** The poems give chorus 1 to a school social worker and chorus 2 to first love, cross-legged in a closet. The answer is word for word the same. Only the second asker can hear it.
- **Her voice opens where there are no words.** The vocalise after each chorus (2:00.3 and 3:48.8 in the master; the second is a backing layer, which Jade keeps because she wants it there live), and the hand taps, which the poems gloss as "that means she wants to say something." The percussion is her.
- **The parents' replies are the brackets.** "(Her English comes from books)" and "For our culture, that's just how it looks" are a defence, and the second is sung over pads alone.
- **The outro softens the poem.** The poem ends "Ugh, I'll never get to think in peace again." The song ends "And I can think again."

### Camera

- **Two positions, in meaning.** *Outside* looks at her face: frontal, level, locked off. Toner labels can land here. *Inside* looks along her eye-line: close, shallow, drifting as her attention drifts.
- **The ratio moves.** The film opens mostly outside and ends inside. Outside shots get shorter in every section.
- **The same framing, repeated, is how the film rhymes.** The diagonal beam four times (attic, wall, Voyager, playground). A hand closing on her forearm three times. Her finger pointing three times. Her mouth opening with no sound twice.

### Directing her face

- Her eyes count, track and jump. When performing an expression, she might over or underestimate the amplitude. That is the performance. Off-stage the expression stays in her face at lower amplitude, never fully wiped.
- Every shot of her gets a playable action, per the canon: counting spirals, reading down a column for the short row, listening for the next tear. The canon's banned words stay banned.
- She grins when an idea delights her and she taps. Both are in the poems and both must be on screen before the first chorus.

### Fifteen percent above amplitude

At the party, and only there, she is drawn at 115%: line a little heavier, wash a little more saturated, gestures a little larger and a few frames early. Guests stay at 100. On "I think he's right. Honestly." she drops back to 100 in one cut.

### Palette and text

- Cream paper, ink greys, gold graphite, toner black. Frost and light are bare paper.
- Colour is rationed: handout yellow, screen blue, the pale blue dot, lemons, fire.
- On-screen text is the place-and-year headers from "First Principles", gold numerals, the toner labels, and three lyric exceptions: "Good catch!" as a toner label attributed to Dad, the caption "artichoke" (2:11.9), and the textbook caption "Did she understand or just pattern match?" under the kitchen illustration (K1, 2:22.2; Jade). ## Production approach

Every setting is built as a 3D set first, in the Chaos Monkey layout engine, and every shot is derived from it. Nothing is generated until the whole film exists as a line-render animatic cut to the song.

### Order of work

1. **Lock the song map** for the master. Done for -2sm (`analysis/m2sm/song_map.md`, 2026-11-25); Jade's ear checks are in, except the cliff (below).
2. **Build the sets** in `layout/sets/` (a copy of the Chaos Monkey layout engine). All exist (`attic88_day` is the daylight rig: sun bearing, frost on the four panes, a reading spot at the sill, the paper keyboard on the boards).
   - **Retime the sets to the master.** Each set's `shots.js` still carries v3 times except a few new mv10 shots. `analysis/m2sm/remap.py --file <shots.js>` shows the change as a dry run; `--write` applies it; `--snap` sends lyric-synced cuts to the sung onsets. Shots it flags (the dropped interlude half, the coda lead-in) are re-placed by hand: the market and kitchen interlude shots, and the spiral plates.
3. **Animatic.** One camera per shot, line renders, cut to the master (`inputs/Proof (-2sm).wav`, full mix) at true timing. This is where the outside-to-inside ratio and the repeated framings get checked.
4. **Look test on one shot:** the attic beam. It has to prove that light can be bare paper, that a mote reads at 1080p, and that her eyes can carry a shot. It also has to show whether code can produce a watercolor-looking wash like the friend's reference (image 5).
5. **Cast.** Age shifts of little Jade to 3, 7 and about 10, and of Jade to about 19 and 27, from the accepted v3 heads. Dad from the likeness Jade approved. First love's face is never shown or drawn (he is a real person): back of head, over-the-shoulder, hands and forearms only. Strangers need costume sheets only.
6. **Stills, then motion,** shot by shot from the line guides.
7. **Renderer layers,** composited on top: beams, motes, gold notes, toner labels, headers.
8. **Sung shots,** then edit, grade and the subtitle track.

### Risks

- **Image models paint light as glow, not as absence.** That is why the beam is a composited mask. The look test settles it.
- **Undrawn faces can read as horror.** They should read as unfinished: jaw and hair in light line, features simply not reached, and usually turned, cropped or out of the beam. Test on the social worker first.
- **The anti-sync shot can read as a mistake.** Put a correctly synced line just before it, hold it long, and let her breathe.
- **Fingers on paper keys** need note-level timing. If it will not hold, shoot the hands wide and let the taps carry it.
- **The unfolding has to fit before the taps.** It is about two seconds at the top of the taps section (0:04.9); if the taps start on the downbeat, the last fold opens over the pad's final second. The map-fold creases and the tape seams between sheets stay on the printout in every later keys shot (piano break, coda), which is what keeps it reading as paper. `attic88_day` has no camera for the unfolding yet.
- **Three new ages of one child** is the biggest consistency load in the cast.
- **The growth footage runs at 3.7x to 7.2x.** Its transitions are slow optical-flow morphs between drawn stages, so they stay smooth when sped up. At 6-7x each stage lasts about a second, though, and the plant reads as being re-engraved more than as time-lapse growth (the footage's own weakness: `media/footage/artichoke/NOTES.md`). If that is too fast, a film cut can be re-rendered with its own timing (`animate.py --timing film.json --tag film`), with transitions spaced to the slots, rather than sped up.
- **The interlude is now growth then count** (2:23.7-2:30.0 growth, 2:30.0-2:34.5 count). The "match" echo starts inside the kitchen question, so the growth begins 1.5 s before the interlude's downbeat (2:25.2), on the echo, as Jade asked.
- **"Good catch!" is the one lyric printed in the picture beside a person.** If the toner label beside Dad reads as a comic speech balloon, drop the speaker tag and let the double take carry the attribution.
- **Two clocks in the sets until they are retimed.** Most `shots.js` files use v3 times; a few newer mv10 shots (the shelf pan, the temple push-in, the room crossing, "nailed it") and the spectrogram plate already use the master. Retime with `remap.py` and read its dry run before `--write`.

Generated sheets only go to outside services. No real photos, per the canon.
