A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A five-year-old girl's own view, looking straight down past her chest at her two small hands playing a paper keyboard that lies on the bare hardwood floorboards of a dusty attic, a winter morning in 1988. We see what she sees: no head, no face. The paper keyboard runs across the whole width of the picture. Above it, old wide floorboards running diagonally. At the bottom edge, close to us and large, her own lap: the hem of her white zip jacket and her crossed legs in loose dark navy trousers, and her two forearms in white jacket sleeves with ribbed cuffs coming in from the lower left and lower right corners to the hands.

THE PRINTOUT. Two sheets of ordinary white printer paper, each laid landscape, joined end to end by ONE strip of clear, slightly shiny sticky tape that crosses the paper from top to bottom at the exact middle; the tape is a little crooked and a little wider than it needs to be. Printed along the paper in slightly grey, slightly uneven toner (a cheap 1980s office printer, not a real instrument): one row of piano keys seen flat, exactly fourteen long white keys divided by thin lines, with short black keys along the far edge in groups of two, three, two, three, as in image 1. Soft creases cross the paper from top to bottom at even intervals where it has been folded up like a road map, so the paper does not lie quite flat: each panel tilts a little and the creases catch a hair of shadow. The far right corner curls up off the floor and casts a small shadow. The paper's edges are slightly dog-eared.

COMPOSITION. Follow image 1 exactly: the same camera straight above, the same size and position of the paper keyboard, the same floorboards, the two hands in the same places near the middle of the lower half, the forearms entering from the two lower corners. Image 1 is only a layout drawing from a 3D model: its hands are crude mitts and blocks, replace them with real child's hands; the big smooth dome at the bottom is her body seen from above with the head removed, replace it with her lap as she herself sees it (jacket hem, crossed navy legs); and do not keep its mechanical even line.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She is playing a tune she knows by heart on the printed keys, both hands at once. Each hand has the trained shape of a small child who has had real piano lessons since before she was three: wrist level and low, knuckles raised into a round arch as if holding a small ball, all five fingers curved, the fingertips (not the flat pads) touching the paper, the thumb resting on its side. The left hand is on the white keys just left of the tape, the right hand just right of it. In the right hand the third finger is pressing its key and the fourth is lifted a little, about to fall; in the left hand the little finger and thumb are down, an open fifth. Five fingers on each hand, short soft child's fingers, tiny nails, dimples over the knuckles.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. Low winter sun rakes across the floor from the upper right. The printout and both hands and forearms lie in the sun: they are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only the thinnest ink outlines, the grey toner of the printed keys, and the warm colour of her skin. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. The floorboards around the printout are under one even transparent cool blue-grey ink wash with the board joints, grain and nail heads showing through it in line: palest where image 2 is light, and at full strength in the two soft diagonal bands where image 2 is dark, which are the shadow of the window's glazing bars lying across the boards (where a band crosses the printout it is only the faintest grey veil, and the hands stay clear of it). Her lap at the bottom is above the light and under the same blue-grey wash, navy showing through. A thin margin of completely clean bare paper surrounds the printout and the hands, so that they seem lit from within. The edge between wash and bare paper is a clean, slightly soft wet edge. A little grey dust lies in the gaps between the boards.

THE ONE THING SHE IS ATTENDING TO: her hands on the paper keys. They are the only things in the picture painted in full local colour and with the finest, most loving line: warm rosy flesh, pinker at the fingertips and knuckles; the tape's faint shine; every printed key drawn. Everything else is line under wash with no colour of its own.
