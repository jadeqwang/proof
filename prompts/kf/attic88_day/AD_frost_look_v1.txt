A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A dusty attic room above a restaurant in the Netherlands, a winter morning in 1988. We look over the right shoulder of a five-year-old Chinese girl who sits on the floor: the back of her head is close to us and large in the lower left of the picture, out of focus in nothing, simply near. Beyond her, in the upper right, the plain gable wall with ONE square window of four panes in an old wooden frame with a wooden sill; a thin closed book lies on the sill. Every pane is frosted. Below the window, on the floor: a leaning stack of nine or ten old cloth-bound textbooks; to its left a flat pillow and the end of a folded quilt on a thin sleeping mat. Upper left: the sloping underside of the roof with one exposed rafter, coming down to a low knee wall. A strip of bare floorboards at the bottom right. Nothing else.

COMPOSITION. Follow image 1 exactly: the same camera, the same perspective, the same positions and sizes of her head, the window, the sill, the book stack, the pillow and the roof slope. Image 1 is only a layout drawing from a 3D model: its big circle in the lower left is the back of the child's head (hair, not a ball), with her right shoulder below it at the bottom edge; its flat boxes are the pillow and the folded quilt (soft, rounded, creased); its stack of slabs is the pile of textbooks; and do not keep its mechanical even line. The curved swags drawn inside the window panes of image 1 are only the upper limit of the frost on the glass: do not draw them as curtains or as hard lines.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. A moment ago she was bent over a paper keyboard on the floor. She has just lifted her head and is looking up at the frost on the window. We see her from behind and slightly to her right: the back and right side of her head, turned a little so that the round curve of her right cheek, the tip of her small nose and the ends of her upper lashes just show beyond the side lock of hair, a lost profile. Her right ear shows through the hair. Her head is perfectly still and tilted up; what we can see of her eye is aimed at the lower right pane of the window. Her mouth is hidden from us.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. Low winter sun comes in through the four panes toward us. The four panes are the brightest thing in the picture: they are NOT PAINTED, bare cream paper left blank, with only the frost drawn on them in the faintest line. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. Everything else is under one even transparent cool blue-grey ink wash, with the room's ink lines showing through it, no other colour, no black; a little deeper under the roof. The wash has two strengths, and image 4 is the map of them: where image 4 is black the wash is at full strength (the roof, the knee wall, the left of the wall, the right edge of the wall, and all of her head and shoulder); where image 4 is white we are looking into the shaft of sunlit air that comes from the window down toward us, and there the wash is only a breath, less than half the strength, so the shaft reads as a paler band with two straight, clean, slightly soft wet edges spreading from the window toward the bottom of the picture. Do not draw rays inside it. Do not draw any dust in the air. Her head is in front of the shaft and stays in full shade.

THE WINDOW AND THE FROST: the one thing she is attending to, drawn with the finest, most loving line in the picture. The wooden frame, the sill and the cross of glazing bars were painted long ago and the paint is chipped and cracked: small irregular flakes missing, a fine network of hairline cracks that does not quite repeat, in the thinnest ink line, under the same blue-grey wash as the wall (no brown). On the glass, frost: on the lower half of each pane a low growth of feathery ice crystals in the faintest pale blue-grey line, densest along the bottom edge and in the lower corners, thinning to nothing upward, the upper half of each pane clear and empty. On the lower right pane ONE frost fern is larger and finer than all the rest: it grows from that pane's lower right corner diagonally up toward the pane's centre, a real dendritic ice crystal, a straight main stem with straight side branches leaving it on both sides at the same sixty-degree angle, each side branch carrying its own smaller side branches at that same angle, needle-sharp tips. A thin margin of completely clean bare paper surrounds that fern.

THE GIRL, from behind. Glossy jet-black hair painted in soft black ink wash with a gloss: a small half-ponytail tied high at the back of the crown with a plain dark band, the rest falling straight to just past her shoulders, long side locks in front of the ears. A big round child's head. A cropped white zip jacket with a stand collar, and one straight horizontal orange band across the upper back and around the upper arm, just visible at the bottom edge, under the blue-grey wash. Her cheek, where it shows, is warm and rosy under the wash, never grey. Image 5 is her model sheet: use its back view and side view for the hair, the ponytail, the ear and the jacket.
