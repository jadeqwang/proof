A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. Inside a bedroom closet, about 2002, in the afternoon, very close. Two people of 19 sit cross-legged facing each other, leaning in. We are beside and a little behind the young man's right shoulder. Of him we see, large, across the left and upper part of the picture: the lower half of his head from behind and to the side, the wavy hair over his ear and at his nape, his cheek, the line of his jaw, his chin, his neck, and the hood lying on his shoulder. The top edge of the picture cuts him off just above the cheekbone: no eye, no brow, no forehead, no bridge of the nose is in the picture. He is holding her right wrist lightly in his right hand and, without noticing that he is doing it, he is rubbing the BACK of her hand slowly against the stubble on his chin and jaw. At the right edge, partly cut by it, the young woman's face, turned toward him. Behind them, the plain wall of the closet and, at the upper left, a few clothes on hangers.

COMPOSITION. Follow image 1 exactly: the same camera, the same perspective, the same size and position of his head and neck (upper left and upper centre), his shoulder (lower left), her forearm coming in from the right, her hand at his chin in the centre of the picture, and her face at the right edge. Image 1 is only a layout drawing from a 3D model: replace its mannequins with the real people and its boxes with the real things, and do not keep its mechanical even line. The slabs at the upper left are clothes on hangers. The small slab on the mannequin's forehead is nothing: she has NO fringe. The mitt at his chin is her right hand. The hands, in words: her hand is limp and open, not a fist. Its BACK, the flat back of the hand between the knuckles and the wrist, together with the backs of her loosely curled, completely relaxed fingers, lies against the side of his chin and along his jaw. Her palm therefore faces away from his face, toward herself and toward us: we see the soft inside of her half-open hand, the pads of her palm, the inner sides of her curled fingers and her thumb lying loose. She is NOT cupping his chin, NOT holding it, NOT propping it on her knuckles, and her fingertips do not touch him. His right hand comes up from the lower left and holds her wrist loosely between his thumb and fingers, moving her hand for her.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She has just told him she does not know, and now she is watching his face while her hand is being rubbed on his chin. It tickles. The corner of her mouth nearest to us has just moved: her lips are closed and one corner has only now begun to lift, a smile that has not arrived yet. Her eyes are on his eyes: both irises point up and to the left, at his face above the top edge of the picture.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The only light is daylight from the door he opened, which is behind him, off the left edge. It comes past his shoulder and falls on HER: her hand, her wrist and forearm in its grey sleeve, his fingers where they hold her wrist, her face and hair, and the wall behind her are in the light. Her face is entirely in the light: no shadow, no band of darkness across any part of it. These lit parts are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. On her lit face the only tones are a faint warm rosy blush on cheek and lips, the soft black ink of her hair and brows and her near-black irises, each with one small crisp catch-light of bare paper shaped like a tall narrow upright rectangle (the open door). He is outside the light, and this must be plain at a glance: his hair, ear, cheek, jaw, neck, hood and shoulder, the whole left half of the picture including the hanging clothes and the wall behind him, lie under ONE deep, even, transparent warm brown-black ink wash, soft, clearly darker than the blank paper on her side. Under it his hair and his hoodie lose their own colours (no green, no chestnut brown: only the brown-black of the wash), with every wave, fold and seam still readable through it as slightly darker line. Never flat black, no bright outline round him. On his cheek and jaw the wash is a thinner glaze over warm skin, never grey, so that the stubble can be read. His stubble is drawn over the wash as many fine, short, separate ink strokes, each one a single hair a millimetre or two long, sparse and patchy on the cheek, denser along the edge of the jaw and on the chin: draw them one by one, do not smudge them into a grey shadow. The edge between the dark wash and the blank paper runs down the picture just in front of his profile and chin, where her hand touches him, as a clean, slightly soft wet edge: left of it everything is washed, right of it everything is blank lit paper.

THE ONE THING SHE IS ATTENDING TO: her hand against his chin. It is the only thing in the picture painted in full local colour, with the finest, most loving line in the picture: warm living flesh, rosier at the knuckles; exactly five fingers, every knuckle and the small creases over each joint, every fingernail, the tendons on the back of the hand, the wrist bone; a slender, smooth young woman's hand with a bare wrist. Leave a thin margin of clean bare paper round it so that it seems lit from within. Everything else in the frame stays line, or line under wash, with no colour of its own.
