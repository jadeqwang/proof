A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. Inside a bedroom closet, about 2002, in the afternoon. We look over the right shoulder of a young man who sits cross-legged on the carpet with his back to us: the back of his wavy head, his neck and his shoulder fill the left half of the picture, large and close. Beyond him, an arm's length away, a young woman of 19 sits facing him and us, level with our eyes, her head and shoulders at the right of centre. Behind her, the plain painted end wall of the closet; at the far left edge, a few clothes on hangers. Nothing else.

COMPOSITION. Follow image 1 exactly: the same camera, the same perspective, the same size and position of the back of his head and his shoulders at the left, and of her head and shoulders at the right of centre, her eyes a little above the middle of the picture. Image 1 is only a layout drawing from a 3D model: replace its mannequins with the real people and its boxes with the real things, and do not keep its mechanical even line. The tall slabs at the left are clothes on hangers. The small slab on the mannequin's forehead is nothing: she has NO fringe, her forehead is open. The round shapes at the bottom are their shoulders and raised forearms: their hands are joined below the bottom edge and are not in the picture.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. He has just asked her, "How do you feel? Inside?" She has looked up from her hands, and she answers him by singing it quietly, almost to herself: "I honestly don't know." We catch her on the last word, "know": her lips are pushed a little forward and rounded into a small open 'oh', exactly the mouth shape (c) 'oh' in the bottom row of image 3, the tips of the upper teeth not showing. Her head is lifted and tipped a few degrees toward her own shoulder. Both her irises point at the left side of the picture and very slightly upward, at his eyes, which are hidden from us behind the back of his head: she is looking straight at him and nowhere else. Her brows are a little lifted, the inner ends highest. It is an open face, with nothing held back, and a little wry: it almost amuses her that she does not know, and she is not afraid to tell him so. She is not posing for anyone.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The only light is daylight from the door he opened, which is behind him and behind us. It comes over his shoulder and falls full on HER. Her whole face is in the light, from hairline to chin and from ear to ear, with her neck, the open front of her hoodie, her T-shirt and the wall directly behind her head: there is no shadow, no band and no blade of darkness across any part of her face. All of this is NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. On her lit face the only tones are a faint warm rosy blush on her cheeks, the tip of her nose and her lips, the soft black ink of her hair and brows, and her near-black irises, each with one small crisp catch-light of bare paper shaped like a tall narrow upright rectangle (the open door), on the same side in both eyes. Her glasses are a thin wire line with clear lenses: no glare, nothing hiding her eyes. He is entirely outside the light: the back of his head, his neck, his hood and his shoulder are a dark-wash shape in the foreground, under one even transparent warm brown-black ink wash, soft, with the waves of his hair and the folds of the hood still readable as line through it, no local colour, never black, no bright outline round him. The hanging clothes and the edges and corners of the closet are under the same wash. Where his dark shape overlaps her lit figure the edge is clean; elsewhere the edge between wash and blank paper is a clean, slightly soft wet edge.
