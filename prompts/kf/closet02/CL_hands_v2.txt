A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. Inside a bedroom closet, about 2002. We look almost straight down, through a young woman's own eyes, at four hands held together between two people who sit cross-legged on a beige carpet, facing each other, knees almost touching. Her two forearms, in the soft heather-grey sleeves of a zip hoodie, come up from the bottom edge of the picture. His two forearms, in the faded forest-green sleeves of a zip hoodie pushed a little way up the wrist, come down from the top edge. In the centre of the picture he holds both of her hands in both of his. At the lower left and lower right, her knees in bootcut jeans; at the upper left and upper right, his knees in loose worn jeans; between the knees, short-pile carpet. Under her, just showing at the bottom corners, the folded edge of a cotton duvet cover she is sitting on. Nothing else.

COMPOSITION. Follow image 1 exactly: the same camera looking down, the same symmetry, the same positions and sizes of the two pairs of forearms (hers from the bottom, his from the top), the four knees and the knot of hands in the middle. Image 1 is only a layout drawing from a 3D model: replace its mannequin limbs with real arms, real knees in real cloth with folds, and its square mitts with real hands, and do not keep its mechanical even line. The two big square blocks in the middle of image 1 are his two hands; the shapes just below them are her two hands. Arrange the four hands as in the study at the top centre of image 3 (four hands seen from above): her two small hands lie side by side with their backs upward, fingers relaxed and a little curled, thumbs showing at the inner sides; his two larger hands hold them from the outside and from underneath, each of his hands cradling one of hers, his fingers under her palms, his two thumbs resting across the backs of her hands just behind her knuckles. All four hands are fully visible, none hidden under another. There is no bracelet on any wrist.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She has just looked up at him and he has taken both her hands; now she looks down at them. This picture is what she sees: nothing but the hands, in complete detail.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. He has opened the closet door behind him and daylight comes in over his shoulder, from the top of the picture. Image 2 shows exactly where it falls (white) and where it does not (black): the light covers the upper part of the picture and the four hands in the centre, and stops just past her wrists; her forearms, her knees and the bottom of the picture are in shade. The lit carpet, his lit sleeves and knees and the paper between the hands are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. The lit carpet is blank paper with at most a few tiny ink flicks for its pile: no stippling, no all-over texture. His lit sleeves and jeans are a few long clean ink fold lines on blank paper: no hatching, no pencil shading. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. Everything in the shade is under one even transparent warm brown-black ink wash, soft, with the folds of her sleeves and jeans showing through as line, no local colour, no black. The edge between light and shade is a clean, slightly soft wet edge.

THE ONE THING SHE IS ATTENDING TO: the four hands. They are the only thing in the picture painted in colour, and they are a LINE DRAWING first: every finger, nail, knuckle and crease is bounded and described by a clear, thin, dark ink contour drawn with the point of a fine brush, the finest and most loving line in the picture, as in a baimiao figure painting and as in the hand studies of image 3. Inside those ink lines the flesh is one pale, flat, transparent warm wash, with a little rose dropped in at the knuckles and fingertips, and the paper grain showing through it. The hands are NOT modelled with shading: no photographic skin, no pores, no soft airbrushed roundness, no cast shadows between the fingers, no coloured-pencil texture. Draw every knuckle. Twenty fingers, exactly five on each hand, each one accounted for and each one anatomically right: the three joints of every finger with their small creases, every fingernail, the tendons fanning on the backs of his hands, his wrist bones, the pads of her palms and the lines across them, the soft web between thumb and forefinger. His hands: a lean white man's hands, large, long-fingered, square-tipped with short nails, prominent knuckles and wrist bone, long lean forearms with fine light-brown hair. Her hands: a young Chinese woman's, clearly smaller, slender, smooth, with bare wrists and no jewellery. Leave a thin margin of clean bare paper round the whole knot of hands, so that they seem lit from within. Everything else in the frame stays line, or line under wash, with no colour of its own.

PEOPLE. Image 3 is the study sheet of his hands and of her hands in his: use it for the shape, size and skin of both people's hands and for the fineness of the line; ignore its blue sleeves and its cord bracelet (in 2002 he wears a faded forest-green hoodie and no bracelet). She wears a soft heather-grey zip-up hoodie and mid-blue bootcut jeans; he wears a faded forest-green zip hoodie and worn light-blue loose jeans; in this picture those clothes are drawn in line only, as described under LIGHT. No faces are in the picture. Use the images for likeness only; repaint in this painting.

Not anime, not cel shading, not a photograph, not a coloured-pencil drawing, not photorealism, not a 3D or digital render, no dramatic chiaroscuro, no black shadows, no rim light. No text, no letters, no numbers, no signature, no seal, no border, no frame. Do not draw any dust in the air.
