A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A narrow walk-in bedroom closet in a shared student flat, about 2002, in the afternoon, seen from high up at one end, looking down its length. Beige short-pile carpet. Along the left wall, a rail of clothes on hangers (shirts, a long coat, a dress, a jacket), hanging close together, with a shelf above them holding folded jumpers and one shoebox. A plain painted end wall. On the right, the white door frame and the plain white closet door, which has just been opened and stands open outward. At the far end, a young woman of 19 sits cross-legged on a folded duvet cover that lies on the carpet (a neat flat rectangle of folded cotton with a faint small check, a few fingers thick), with her elbows on her knees and her head in her hands: her face is down and hidden in her palms, her fingers pushed up into her hairline, her long black hair falling forward past her knees. In the foreground a young man of 19, seen from behind and above, is in the act of lowering himself to sit on the carpet in front of her: he is caught half-way down in a low crouch, knees bent and apart, his left hand reaching down to the carpet to steady himself, his weight not yet on the floor, his head bowed toward her. We see only his back, the hood lying flat between his shoulder blades and the back of his wavy head.

COMPOSITION. The tall slabs along the left are the hanging clothes and the shelf above them. The tall narrow panel at the right is the open door and its frame; open it wider than in image 1, so that it is plainly a door someone has just pulled open. She is bowed forward with her head in her hands. He is half-way down, crouching, as described.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The only light is the daylight from the bedroom, coming through the door he has just opened, behind his right shoulder. It falls as ONE diagonal wedge from the top of the door opening at the upper right down toward the lower left, at about 40 degrees: image 2 shows the direction and the slope of this light (its white shape), but in the painting the wedge is about three times as wide as in image 2, wide enough to hold her whole seated figure. The wedge passes over his right shoulder without touching him and lands on HER: her bowed head and hair, her hands, her hoodie, her knees and feet, the folded duvet cover, and the patch of carpet and end wall directly round her. Everything inside the wedge is NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. On her the only tones are the soft black ink of her hair and the faintest warm flesh on her fingers. Everything the wedge does not touch is under one even transparent warm brown-black ink wash, soft, a little deeper among the hanging clothes and in the near corners, with the lines of the clothes, hangers, shelf and carpet showing through it and no local colour. He is entirely outside the light: a dark-wash back in the foreground, the same warm brown-black wash a little deeper over him, the folds of his hoodie and the waves of his hair still readable in line through it, never black, no bright outline round him. The wedge has two straight edges, and the edge between wash and blank paper is a clean, slightly soft wet edge.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She does not know yet that the door has opened. She sits folded over, quite still, head in her hands, thinking and getting nowhere. She is drawn with the finest line in the picture.

An invented character, not a likeness of any real person. He is never shown face-on: we see no part of his face. Use the images for likeness only; repaint in this painting.
