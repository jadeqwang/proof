A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A housewarming party in Mountain View, California, 2010, at dusk, on the patio behind the house. The host, a 27-year-old Chinese woman, is hugging a guest. We are close, level with their heads. The guest stands with her back to us, just right of the centre of the picture, large: the back of her head with one long braid, her shoulders and back. The host faces us on the far side of the hug: her chin rests over the guest's shoulder, so her whole face is visible just left of the guest's head, and her two arms come round onto the guest's back. Behind them is the back wall of the house. At the left, the wide sliding glass door stands open, and through it we see into the living room: a wall of bookshelves full of books, one guest standing by them with a cup, and the corner of a sofa. At the right edge of the frame another guest bends over a garden table that holds a glass jug and a row of cups. On the house wall at the upper right fall the soft shadows of a lemon tree: leaves and a few very large round fruit.

COMPOSITION. Follow image 1 exactly: the same camera, the same perspective, the same positions and sizes of her face in the centre, the guest's head and back to the right of it, the two arms across the lower middle of the picture, the door frame and the room at the left, the bending guest and table at the right edge. Image 1 is only a layout drawing from a 3D model: replace its mannequins with the real people and its boxes with real things (the small boxes at the right are cups and a jug; the boxes inside the room are a sofa and a side table), and do not keep its mechanical even line. The mannequin's arms are stiff tubes: paint a real, close hug instead. Her hand on the right is laid flat on the guest's upper back between the shoulder blades, fingers together, lifted a finger's width off the cloth in the middle of a pat; her other hand rests lower on the guest's back. Five fingers on each hand. The guest's arms go round the host's waist.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. With her body she is giving a warm, big, well-made hug: arms wrapped a little higher and tighter than a hug between acquaintances needs, up on her toes, shoulders lifted, about fifteen per cent more than the moment calls for. With her face, which the guest cannot see, she is counting how long the hug has lasted. Her head is perfectly still, chin on the shoulder. Her lips are together and level, her jaw loose, her brows resting. She is not smiling and not frowning and not posing for anyone: all the life in the face is in the eyes, which are wide open and turned down and away to the LEFT side of the picture, both irises in the lower left corners of the eyes, fixed and bright on a spot on the patio floor, the way a child looks when she is counting. One pat for each count.

SHE IS DRAWN LOUDER THAN EVERYONE ELSE. She alone is drawn with a slightly heavier, darker ink outline (about fifteen per cent thicker than every other line in the picture) and with washes one step richer: a deep indigo navy dress, blacker hair, warm rosy skin, a little more colour in cheeks and lips. Image 3 shows this: its LEFT figure is the ordinary drawing, its RIGHT figure is how she is drawn here. She is the only thing in the picture in full local colour, with a thin margin of clean bare paper round her head and hands. Everyone and everything else is thin, pale, even line with no colour of its own.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The sun is very low behind us and to the left; the yard fence shades the patio, and the last sun passes over it onto the upper part of the house wall, onto her face, and in through the open door. Image 2 shows it. The room seen through the open door, with its bookshelves, and the sunlit upper wall, are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines for the books, shelves and furniture. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. Her face and hands are in that last light and are painted normally with no wash over them: warm rosy flesh colour, the pink blush on her cheeks clear and fresh, never grey, never ashen. Everything else, the door frame, the lower wall, the patio, the guest's back and braid, the bending guest and the table, is under one even transparent dusk-violet ink wash, the ink lines showing through, with no other colour in it; the shadows of the lemon tree on the wall are the same violet wash with a clean, slightly soft wet edge. No black anywhere. In each of her eyes one small crisp catch-light of bare paper, a low horizontal sliver like the last of the sun, on the same side in both eyes.

PEOPLE. Jade, 2010, age 27: a Chinese woman; an oval face with a softly pointed chin, almond eyes with deep near-black irises (almost as dark as her hair), dark, fairly strong, slightly asymmetric eyebrows (the eyebrow on the LEFT of the picture is straight; the eyebrow on the RIGHT of the picture runs straight and then lifts into a soft arch), a straight nose, full lips. Thin dark rectangular half-rim glasses. Long black hair with NO fringe: an open forehead, parted slightly off-centre, swept to both sides in long soft face-framing layers with a loose wave, past the shoulders. A dark navy jersey dress with three-quarter sleeves. Image 4 is her accepted face sheet: reproduce THAT woman's face, glasses and hair exactly; keep her natural, real face. Image 5 is her pose sheet: its lower middle figure is this hug (her face over the guest's shoulder, eyes open and looking away).
The guests (image 6 is their costume sheet; use it for clothes only, all under the violet wash here, without the sheet's colours). The hugged guest, from behind: a woman with one long braid down her back, a fleece vest over a striped long-sleeved top. The guest inside the room: a man in a hoodie with a cup. The guest at the right edge: a man in a plaid flannel shirt, bending over the table. NO GUEST HAS A FACE: wherever a guest's head is turned enough to show a face, it is a simple shape under the same wash as what is behind it, with the hairline, ear and jaw in thin line and no features, like a figure the painter has not reached yet. Not a mask, not a white oval, not a blur. Only the host has eyes, a nose and a mouth.
