A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. One face on an otherwise empty sheet of paper. A 27-year-old Chinese woman, seen frontally, level with her eyes, from very near: her head and shoulders in the middle of the frame, cut off at the chest by the bottom edge. There is NO room, NO background, NO bookshelf, NO shadow behind her, NO ground, NO horizon: everything round her is the bare cream paper, completely blank from edge to edge, the same paper as her highlights.

COMPOSITION. Image 2 is the previous frame of the same film: the same woman, in the same place, at the same size, a moment earlier, in front of her bookshelves. Paint her head, hair, neck and shoulders in exactly the same position and at exactly the same size as in image 2 (and as the mannequin in image 1, the layout drawing), the same hair, the same glasses, the same necklace, and REMOVE EVERYTHING ELSE: every shelf, every book, every shadow, every bit of violet wash is gone, as if the room had been lifted off the page. Do not keep the mechanical even line of image 1.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She has just finished singing one word and has closed her mouth, and she is looking at us to see what we make of it. Her head is perfectly still and squared to us, chin level. Her lips are together and level, her jaw loose, her brows resting. She is not smiling and not frowning and not posing for anyone: all the life in the face is in the eyes, which are fixed and bright exactly on the lens, both irises centred, wide open, steady, the way a child looks when she is counting. She is waiting, and she is not going to add anything.

HOW IT IS PAINTED. Only her face is fully painted: warm rosy flesh colour in thin transparent washes, the pink blush on her cheeks clear and fresh, the soft modelling under the brows, beside the nose and under the lower lip and chin, the lips, the dark irises, the thin dark glasses, never grey, never ashen. Her hair is soft black ink wash with a gloss, exactly as in image 2. Below the chin the painting thins out: the neck in the palest flesh wash, the thin chain and its small round pale-blue pendant, and then the scoop neck and shoulders of her dress given only as a few thin ink outlines with a breath of navy wash that fades to nothing before the bottom edge, like a drawing the painter stopped. No wash anywhere else on the sheet. No cast shadow, no halo, no vignette, no tone round her head: the hair meets clean bare paper. In each eye one small crisp catch-light of bare paper, on the same side in both eyes.

HER. Jade, 2010, age 27: a Chinese woman; an oval face with a softly pointed chin, almond eyes with deep near-black irises (almost as dark as her hair), dark, fairly strong, slightly asymmetric eyebrows (the eyebrow on the LEFT of the picture is straight; the eyebrow on the RIGHT of the picture runs straight and then lifts into a soft arch), a straight nose, full lips. Thin dark rectangular half-rim glasses. Long black hair with NO fringe: an open forehead, parted slightly off-centre, swept to both sides in long soft face-framing layers with a loose wave, past the shoulders. Image 3 is her accepted face sheet: reproduce THAT woman's face exactly, as in its first portrait (top left, front view, lips closed); keep her natural, real face, do not idealise her. Use the images for likeness only; repaint her in this painting.
