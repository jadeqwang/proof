A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A housewarming party in a small house in Mountain View, California, 2010, at dusk, the lamps just switched on. A 27-year-old Chinese woman, the host, stands with her back to a wall of bookshelves, facing us, seen level with her eyes from two metres away, from the waist up. Beside her at the right edge of the frame stands a tall young man with his back to us, reading the book spines. Behind them the wall is all books: at the left and centre a tall dark-wood bookcase filled edge to edge with uniform sets of thick reference volumes, and on one shelf beside her head a short run of small slim paperbacks; at the far right, behind the man, the start of a second, pale bookcase with books of every height, some leaning, some stacked flat. No book has a title: every spine is blank or carries at most one small abstract band or dot.

COMPOSITION. Follow image 1 exactly: the same camera, the same perspective, the same positions and sizes of her head and shoulders (centre, a little left), her arm reaching across to the right, the man's back and head at the right edge, the shelves and the upright where the two bookcases meet. Image 1 is only a layout drawing from a 3D model: replace its mannequins with the real people and its boxes with real books, and do not keep its mechanical even line. Her arm at frame right is a real bare forearm and hand: her whole open hand, palm down, fingers together, lies flat across the man's bare left forearm just below his pushed-up sleeve, caught in the middle of a fast pat. Five fingers. Her other arm hangs at her side.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She is patting his arm fast with her whole hand, the way she does when an idea has just delighted her and she cannot wait to say it, and she is singing the word "see" straight to us. Her head is squared to us and tipped a few degrees toward him. Both irises point exactly into the lens, wide and shining. Her eyebrows are up. Her mouth is open on a long "ee" with a real, wide grin in it: the lips stretched wide and parted, the corners pulled up and back, the upper teeth showing as one clean simple pale band, a thin dark gap under them, the lower lip full. The mouth shape is clean, simple and easy to read: no extra lines around it. Her cheeks are lifted by the grin and push up under the glasses.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. Three paper floor lamps stand behind us and light her, the man and the fronts of the books; everything the lamps do not reach is the dusk. Image 2 shows it: white and pale = lamp light, grey = shade. The lamp-lit book spines, and above all the whole pale bookcase at the right, are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines for each book. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. On the dark bookcase behind her, the lamps throw soft shadows of her head and shoulders and of his onto the spines, to the left of her, as in image 2: each shadow is one even transparent dusk-violet ink wash laid across the bare-paper books, the book outlines showing through it, with a clean, slightly soft wet edge. The dark-wood shelves and uprights, the gaps above the books and the far corners are under the same dusk-violet wash, a little deeper. No black anywhere. She and he stand in the lamp light, so they are painted normally with no wash over them: her warm rosy flesh colour and the pink blush on her cheeks clear and fresh, never grey, never ashen. In each eye one small crisp catch-light of bare paper, a soft upright oblong like a paper lamp shade, on the same side in both eyes.

THE ONE THING IN FULL COLOUR is her: face, hair, hand and dress carry the finest, most careful line in the picture. The books stay line on bare paper or line under violet wash, with no local colour of their own.
