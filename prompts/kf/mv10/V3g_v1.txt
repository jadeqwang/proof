A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A housewarming party in the living room of a small house in Mountain View, California, 2010, at dusk, seen from high in one corner of the room, looking down across the whole room. The upper quarter of the picture is the plain low ceiling. Along the left wall stand three identical paper floor lamps on thin black stems, each with a plain cylindrical paper shade, evenly spaced, the nearest one cut by the left edge. On the far wall hang two small framed pictures (plain, nothing readable in them) and, right of them, an open doorway to a hall. Three guests stand talking in a loose group in front of that doorway. Along the right wall a low sofa with square arms, one guest sitting on it, and a small round side table at the right edge. An oak floor with a large plain rug in the middle. In the middle of the room, alone on the rug, the host, a 27-year-old Chinese woman, is crossing the room toward us and to the left, in mid-stride, carrying a tall glass of lemonade. In the left foreground, close to us and seen from above and behind, a guest stands with his back to us. In the right foreground, also seen from above and behind, stands a tall young man who has turned his head to watch her go.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She is the host and she is playing a host very well. She walks with a stride a little longer than the room needs, her back very straight, her chin up. Her forward hand (toward the left of the picture) already holds the glass of lemonade out in front of her at chest height, arm nearly straight, offered a few steps too early to a guest who is still across the room; her other arm swings back a little too wide, the hand open. Five fingers on each hand. Her face is turned toward the lower left, toward the guest she is heading for: eyebrows up, eyes wide and fixed on him, a bright, ready, closed-lipped host's smile held a little more firmly than the moment needs. It is all about fifteen per cent bigger than anyone else in the room, and exact, like a part played well.

SHE IS DRAWN LOUDER THAN THE ROOM, and this is the point of the picture. She alone is drawn with a slightly heavier, darker ink outline (about fifteen per cent thicker than every other line in the picture) and with washes one step richer: a deep indigo navy dress, a bright orange belt, blacker hair, warm rosy skin, a little more colour in cheeks and lips. Image 3 shows this: its LEFT figure is the ordinary drawing, its RIGHT figure is how she is drawn here. She and her glass are the only things in the picture in full local colour: the lemonade is a clear pale lemon yellow with one round slice of lemon in it, and there is a thin margin of clean bare paper round her whole figure so that she seems lit from within. Not a caricature: the same woman, the same proportions, simply turned up.
Everyone else is drawn at ordinary strength: thin, pale, even line and no colour of their own, standing slack and ordinary, weight on one leg, cups held low, heads tipped.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. It is dusk. Image 2 shows where light falls (its pale and white shapes) and where it does not (its greys). The last low sun comes in through a big sliding door behind us and lands in two places only: a patch on the rug and floor round her feet, and the far right wall beside the doorway, above the sofa. Those two sunlit patches and the three paper lamp shades are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom, no halo round the lamps: light is simply where the brush did not go. Everything else, ceiling, walls, floor, rug, furniture and all the guests, is under one even transparent dusk-violet ink wash, a little deeper in the corners and under the ceiling, the ink lines showing through it, with no other colour in it. The edge between wash and bare paper is a clean, slightly soft wet edge. On the sunlit right wall the guests by the doorway throw soft violet shadows. No black anywhere.

PEOPLE. Jade, 2010, age 27: a Chinese woman; an oval face with a softly pointed chin, almond eyes with deep near-black irises, dark, fairly strong eyebrows, a straight nose, full lips. Thin dark rectangular half-rim glasses. Long black hair with NO fringe: an open forehead, parted slightly off-centre, swept to both sides in long soft layers with a loose wave, past the shoulders. A dark navy jersey dress to just above the knee with a scoop neck and three-quarter sleeves, a narrow orange belt at the waist, a thin chain with one small round pale-blue pendant, black opaque tights, black ballet flats. The tall man at lower right is her first love, aged 27, seen from above and behind: warm dark-brown hair in loose soft waves, a little shaggy, curling at the nape, a clear whorl at the crown; a heathered slate-blue henley shirt with the sleeves pushed up to just below the elbows, dark indigo jeans; a woven brown cord bracelet on his LEFT wrist. We see the back and top of his head, one ear and the far line of his cheek at most: no eye, no nose, no mouth. Image 5 is his sheet from behind. He is under the violet wash like the room.
The guests (image 6 is their costume sheet; use it for clothes and shoes, all under the violet wash here, without the sheet's colours). At lower left, back to us: a round-shouldered man in a wrinkled oxford shirt with rolled sleeves and corduroys, a plain cup in one hand. By the doorway: a short broad bearded man in a plaid flannel shirt, kilt and hiking boots; a woman in a cardigan over a dotted dress, with a chin-length bob; a slight man in a dark blazer and jeans. NO GUEST HAS A FACE: each guest's head is a simple shape under the same wash as the wall behind it, with the hairline, ear and jaw in thin line and no features, like figures the painter has not reached yet. Not masks, not white ovals, not blurs. Only the host has eyes, a nose and a mouth.
