A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A living room in a small house in Mountain View, California, 2010, during a housewarming party at dusk, the lamps on. We stand behind a 27-year-old Chinese woman and look past her at what she is looking at: a wall of bookshelves, and on it three shadows of herself. She stands at the right of the frame with her back to us, seen from the thighs up, arms hanging at her sides, perfectly still. The wall in front of her is a tall dark-wood bookcase of three bays and six shelves, running from the left edge to just past her, filled edge to edge with uniform sets of thick reference volumes all the same height; on one shelf at her head height, just left of her head, a short run of small slim paperbacks. At the far right, behind and beyond her, the start of a second, pale bookcase with books of every height, some leaning, some stacked flat, and one small round vase. In the lower left corner a small round side table and the arm of a sofa. A strip of plain ceiling above. No book has a title: every spine is blank or carries at most one thin band.

COMPOSITION. Follow image 1 exactly: the same camera, the same perspective, the same positions and sizes of her figure at the right, the dark bookcase, the pale bookcase behind her, the side table. Image 1 is only a layout drawing from a 3D model: replace its mannequin with the real woman and its boxes with real books and furniture, and do not keep its mechanical even line. The three looping outlines drawn on the bookcase in image 1 are NOT wires or lines: they are the outlines of three shadows of her. Paint them as soft shadows with no outline at all, exactly in those three places and at that size.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She has stopped in front of her own shelf and is comparing the three shadows, one after another, the way one counts. Her head is level and turned very slightly to the left, toward the middle shadow. We see only the back of her head, her hair, her shoulders and back, and a little of the line of her left cheek. Her hands hang open and relaxed, five fingers each.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. Three identical paper floor lamps stand in a row behind us, out of frame, and light the fronts of the books; everything the lamps do not reach is the dusk. Image 2 shows it: pale = lamp light, darker = shadow. The lamp-lit book spines are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines for each book. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go.
THE THREE SHADOWS are what the picture is about and are painted with more care than anything else in it. Each lamp throws one shadow of her onto the book spines, so there are exactly THREE, side by side, all to the LEFT of her, evenly stepped: the same silhouette three times, the same height as she is, standing on the same line: a head with long loose hair, a neck, sloping shoulders, arms hanging, the waist, the flare of a knee-length skirt, two legs running down to the bottom shelf. The three are nearly identical, like three prints from one block; only the one furthest left is very slightly wider than the one nearest her. Their heads are fully separate, with bare-paper books between them; their shoulders and arms overlap a little, and where two overlap the wash is one step deeper. Each shadow is one even, flat, transparent dusk-violet ink wash laid across the bare-paper books, the book outlines showing through it, with a clean, slightly soft wet edge and no outline. Count them: three shadows, no more, no fewer; she herself is a fourth, solid figure at the right.
Everything else that is not lit, the dark-wood shelves and uprights, the gaps above the books, the ceiling, the side table, the sofa arm, the far corners, is under the same transparent dusk-violet wash, a little paler than the shadows, line showing through, with no local colour. No black anywhere.

HER. The lamps are behind her and behind us, so her back is in the light and is painted normally, with no wash over it. Jade, 2010, age 27: a Chinese woman; long black hair with a loose wave, past the shoulders; a dark navy jersey dress to just above the knee with three-quarter sleeves, a narrow orange belt that goes all the way round the waist, black opaque tights.
