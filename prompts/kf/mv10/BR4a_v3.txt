A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A housewarming party in a small house in Mountain View, California, 2010, at dusk, the lamps just switched on. One whole wall of a living room seen square-on, like an elevation: it is all bookshelves, in two halves that meet on the centre line of the picture. LEFT HALF, "truth": a tall dark-wood bookcase of three bays and six shelves, filled edge to edge with uniform sets of thick reference volumes, all the same height, shelf after shelf, like encyclopaedias, handbooks and bound journals; and on one shelf at head height, next to the centre line, one short run of about a dozen small slim science-fiction paperbacks, visibly shorter and thinner than everything around them. RIGHT HALF, "beauty": a pale painted bookcase of three bays, with novels and art books of every height and thickness, some leaning, some stacked flat, gaps between them, and here and there a small round vase or a smooth stone on a shelf. Three people stand in front of the shelves with their backs to us. A 27-year-old Chinese woman, the host, stands just left of the centre line showing the shelf: both arms opened wide, her left hand held out flat toward the truth half and her right hand held out flat toward the beauty half, palms open toward the books, like someone presenting two halves of one idea. To the right of her a tall young man stands in front of the beauty half reading spines, arms at his sides. Further right a second young man, her childhood friend, in a plain hoodie, has one hand up to a book spine, about to pull it out. At the far right edge a sliver of another guest. In the lower left corner the arm of a sofa and a small round side table with nothing on it (no flowers, no plant). A strip of plain ceiling above, an oak floor below.

THE BOOKS ARE THE ONE THING IN FULL COLOUR, drawn with the finest, most loving line in the picture: every spine a separate hand-drawn book with its own slight lean, its own width, soft worn corners, a hint of the top edge of the pages. NO spine has a title or any writing: spines are blank, or carry at most one small abstract mark, a thin band, a dot or a short bar. Both halves are equally LIGHT in value: the cool half is not darker than the warm half, only cooler; its books are pale, clear, luminous watercolour, never dark, never murky, never near-black. The left half is painted entirely in COOL transparent washes: slate blue, blue-grey, deep teal, grey-green, ink blue, in long matching runs, set by set; the paperbacks among them are cool too but brighter and more varied: cobalt, turquoise, violet, pale silver-blue. The right half is painted entirely in WARM transparent washes: ochre, terracotta, rose, peach, brick red, warm brown, cream, no two neighbours alike. The colours are thin watercolour washes with the paper showing through, never opaque, never garish. A thin margin of clean bare paper round the tops of the books on each shelf, so the shelves seem lit from within.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. Three paper floor lamps stand behind us and light the fronts of the books; everything else is the dusk. Image 2 shows it: white and pale = lamp light, grey = shade. Where the lamp light falls on the pale bookcase's frame and shelves, the paper is NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper with only thin ink outlines. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. The three lamps throw THREE nearly identical shadows of her onto the spines of the truth half, side by side, stepped evenly to the left of her, exactly where image 1 draws their outlines and image 2 shows them: each is the same silhouette of her head, shoulders and one outstretched arm, in one even transparent dusk-violet ink wash laid over the coloured books, the books showing through, with a clean, slightly soft wet edge; where two shadows overlap the wash is one step deeper. The ceiling, the side wall, the floor, the sofa and side table are all under one even transparent dusk-violet ink wash, line showing through, with no local colour: the floor is NOT brown and NOT wood-coloured, it is the same grey-violet wash as the ceiling with only thin ink lines for the boards; the dark bookcase's frame is the same violet wash, a little deeper, not brown. Apart from her, the two men and the books, nothing in the picture has any colour but that violet. No black anywhere.
