A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A back yard in Mountain View, California, 2010, at dusk, during a housewarming party. Two people stand under a lemon tree whose lemons are enormous, each the size of a pomelo, as big as a head. We look over the shoulder of a tall young man who stands in the left foreground with his back to us, large: the back of his head, his neck, his shoulders and upper back fill the left-centre of the frame. He has just said something to the woman facing him, and one of his hands is still open, palm up, between them, low in the frame. She is the host, a 27-year-old Chinese woman; she stands right of centre, facing him and us, seen from the waist up. The trunk of the lemon tree rises behind and between them; a cluster of the huge lemons hangs at the upper left beside his head and a few more hang above and behind her. Behind everything a tall plain board fence runs across, its top a straight line a third of the way down the picture, with evening sky above it and lawn below.

COMPOSITION. Follow image 1 exactly: the same camera, the same perspective, the same positions and sizes of his head and back at the left, her head and body right of centre, the round lemons, the trunk, the fence top and the lawn line. Image 1 is only a layout drawing from a 3D model: replace its mannequins with the real people and its spheres with real lemons on real twigs with leaves, and do not keep its mechanical even line. Hands are real hands, five fingers each. She holds nothing.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. He has just told her how the last hour went, and she is checking it: she is running back through the whole hour in her head, every greeting, every hug, every crossing of the room, one after another. Her chin has dipped in a small nod and stayed down. Her eyes are open and turned down and away to the RIGHT side of the picture, both irises in the lower right corners of the eyes, fixed on nothing in the yard, held there, the way a person looks when she is reading down a long column of figures in her head. Her lips are together and level, her jaw loose, her brows resting. She is not smiling and not frowning and not posing for anyone: all the life in the face is in the eyes. One hand, the one on the left of the picture, is raised in front of her chest and has stopped there in mid-air: open, relaxed, the fingers slightly apart and slightly curled, the palm turned half down, paused like a conductor's hand in the moment before the first beat. Her other arm hangs at her side.

HOW SHE IS DRAWN. She is still drawn a little louder than everything round her: a slightly heavier, darker ink outline (about fifteen per cent thicker than his) and washes one step richer: a deep indigo navy dress, a bright orange belt, blacker hair. Image 4 shows this: its LEFT figure is the ordinary drawing, its RIGHT figure is how she is drawn here. He is drawn at ordinary strength, thin even line, quiet wash.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The sun has gone down behind the fence. Image 2 shows it: nearly everything is shade, and only the lemons, which hang above the fence's shadow, still catch the last light. The whole picture, sky, fence, lawn, tree, both people, is under one even transparent dusk-violet ink wash, the fence deeper, the sky palest, the ink lines showing through it and no other colour in it. On her face the wash is a thin glaze laid OVER a normally painted face: her warm rosy flesh colour and the pink blush on her cheeks still show clearly through it, never grey, never ashen. In each eye one small crisp catch-light of bare paper, a tiny round point like a far-off flame, on the same side in both eyes. No yellow light, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom. No black anywhere.

THE LEMONS are the one thing in the picture in full local colour, with the finest, most loving line: each one huge, heavy, slightly lumpy and lopsided, with a blunt nipple at the end, the pitted skin drawn with tiny dots, hanging from a bent twig with two or three dark pointed leaves; a clear lemon yellow wash, paler on the upper left of each fruit where the last sun touches it and the paper is left almost bare, and a thin margin of clean bare paper round each lemon so that they seem lit from within. Nothing else in the picture is yellow.

PEOPLE. Jade, 2010, age 27: a Chinese woman; an oval face with a softly pointed chin, almond eyes with deep near-black irises (almost as dark as her hair), dark, fairly strong, slightly asymmetric eyebrows (the eyebrow on the LEFT of the picture is straight; the eyebrow on the RIGHT of the picture runs straight and then lifts into a soft arch), a straight nose, full lips. Thin dark rectangular half-rim glasses. Long black hair with NO fringe: an open forehead, parted slightly off-centre, swept to both sides in long soft face-framing layers with a loose wave, past the shoulders. A dark navy jersey dress with a scoop neck and three-quarter sleeves, a narrow orange belt at the waist, a thin chain with one small round pale-blue pendant. No logos, no lettering, no pattern. First love, 2010, aged 27: a white American man, lean and tall; warm dark-brown hair in loose soft waves, a little shaggy, covering the tops of the ears and curling at the nape over the collar, a clear whorl at the crown; a long neck; ears that stand out slightly. A heathered slate-blue henley shirt with a plain round neckband, the sleeves pushed up to just below the elbows. He is seen from directly behind: the back of his head, one ear, his nape and shoulders. His face is never seen: no eye, no nose, no mouth.
