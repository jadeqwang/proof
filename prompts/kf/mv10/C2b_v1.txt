A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A living room in Mountain View, California, 2010, late in a housewarming party, night outside, the lamps on. Two people stand close together facing each other, and we look steeply down on them from just above and behind the man's left shoulder. He is at the right: the back and top of his bowed head fill the upper right of the frame, large and near, and below it his shoulder, his back and his left arm. His left forearm is held across in front of him at waist height, the sleeve pushed up to the elbow, and that hand holds a plain glass tumbler of lemonade. She is at the left, a 27-year-old Chinese woman, the host, a head shorter: we see the top of her head and her face from above in three-quarter view, tipped up toward him, then her shoulders, and her right arm reaching across the lower left of the frame to the centre of the picture, where her whole open hand lies flat on his bare forearm, just above the wrist. He is looking straight down at that hand. Below and behind them the oak floor and the corner of a plain rug. At the left edge, in steep perspective, the end of a pale bookcase with a few leaning books and a small round vase. At the top right, on the floor, the round bases and thin stems of two floor lamps. At the top centre, far off, the legs of two other guests.

HER HAND: a slender right hand, palm down, laid flat across the top of his forearm between his wrist and elbow, the four fingers together and straight, the thumb alongside, the fingertips lifted a finger's width off his skin in the middle of a quick pat. Five fingers. HIS HAND: a large left hand closed round the tumbler, five fingers, a woven brown cord bracelet on that wrist.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. Her voice will not work, so she is saying it with her hand: patting his arm quickly with her whole hand, the way she always has when there is something she needs to say. Her face is turned up to his. Her mouth is open a little, lips parted about a finger's width, soft, shaped round no word, a breath held; no sound is coming. Her eyes are on his face, both irises turned up and toward the right of the picture; they are very bright and wet, a thin clear line of water standing along each lower lid and not fallen. Her brows are lifted a little at their inner ends. She is not smiling and not crying.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. Three paper floor lamps stand along the wall at the upper right and light the two of them from that side; everything else is the night. Image 2 shows it. The lamp-lit front of the pale bookcase at the left edge is NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. The floor, the rug, the lamp bases and stems and the far guests' legs are under one even transparent dusk-violet ink wash, the ink lines showing through, with no local colour, and the two people throw one soft deeper violet shadow across the floor toward the lower left. No black anywhere. Her face is in the lamp light and is painted normally: warm rosy flesh colour, the pink blush on her cheeks clear and fresh, never grey, never ashen. In each of her eyes one crisp catch-light of bare paper, a soft upright oblong like a paper lamp shade, on the same side in both eyes.

THE ONE THING BEING LOOKED AT is her hand on his forearm: it is painted in full local colour with the finest, most loving line in the picture: her smooth warm hand, every knuckle and nail; his long lean forearm with its fine light-brown hairs, the wrist bone, the braided cord bracelet strand by strand; and a thin margin of clean bare paper round the hand and forearm so that they seem lit from within. His shirt, her dress and both heads of hair are painted quietly in thin pale washes. The lemonade in the tumbler is the palest lemon yellow.
