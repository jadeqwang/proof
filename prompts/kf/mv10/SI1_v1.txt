A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A close portrait at night in a back yard in Mountain View, California, 2010, late in a housewarming party. A 27-year-old Chinese woman, the host, has turned away from the party to face us, seen frontally, level with her eyes, from very near: her head and shoulders fill the middle of the frame, cut off below the collarbones by the bottom edge. Far behind her, at the end of the lawn, someone is spinning fire poi, and the two rings of fire stand in the dark behind her head like two large hoops, one on each side of her, each partly hidden by her head and shoulders: we see the outer arc of each ring and one small flame on each, the left flame low beside her shoulder, the right flame higher, level with her cheek. Behind the rings a tall plain board fence runs straight across, with one fence post at the left; above it a band of night sky. Nothing else.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. The party voice has gone out of her. She is saying something small and true, almost under her breath, to us alone: the word "right". Her head is still, her chin a little lowered, her shoulders dropped and soft. Her eyes have come up to the lens and rest on it, both irises exactly on us, open, quiet, a little tired, not asking for anything. Her lips are just parted on the long "i" of "right": the jaw dropped only a little, the mouth open no wider than a fingertip, soft and unstretched, the edge of the upper teeth barely showing, the corners neither up nor down. The mouth shape is clean, simple and easy to read. Her brows are resting. She is not smiling and not frowning.

HOW SHE IS DRAWN. Quietly: the thinnest, lightest ink line in the film, pale transparent washes, nothing emphasised, no gesture. Image 5 shows the difference: its RIGHT figure is the louder way she was drawn a moment ago at the party; its LEFT figure, thin line and pale quiet wash, is how she is drawn here.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The sun is gone and the fire behind her is the only light. FIRE IS BARE PAPER. Image 2 shows it: its white shapes are the two arcs and the two flames. Each arc is a narrow ribbon of clean bare cream paper, NOT PAINTED, edged along both sides with a thin line of transparent orange wash that feathers a little outward, a little broken and tapering behind its flame, as a real trail of fire is. Each flame is a small teardrop of bare paper with the same thin orange edge. That orange is the only strong colour in the picture. No yellow fill, no white paint, no glow, no halo, no rays, no haze, no bloom, no sparks, no smoke: the fire is simply where the brush did not go, with an orange edge.
Everything else is in shade under one even transparent dusk-violet ink wash, deeper than at dusk but still transparent, the ink lines showing through: the fence deepest, the sky a little paler. No black anywhere. Her face is turned away from the fire, so it is in shade too: the wash is a thin glaze laid OVER a normally painted face: her warm rosy flesh colour and the pink blush on her cheeks still show clearly through it, never grey, never ashen. Along the outer edge of her hair on both sides, a hair's breadth of bare paper where the fire behind her catches it, no wider. In each eye one small crisp catch-light of bare paper, a tiny round point, on the same side in both eyes.
