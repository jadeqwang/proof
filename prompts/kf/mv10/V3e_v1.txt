A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A back yard in Mountain View, California, 2010, at dusk, during a housewarming party. In the far corner of the lawn, in front of a tall plain board fence, a young man is spinning fire poi: two small burning wicks on thin chains, one in each hand, whirled so fast that each one draws a complete ring of fire in the air. There are exactly TWO rings, standing upright side by side like two great hoops, one on each side of his body, overlapping a little in front of him. He is lean and barefoot, in a sleeveless dark top and loose rolled linen trousers, his hair tied up in a knot. He stands with his feet apart on the grass, arms out from his sides, elbows low, each hand gripping a short handle. A fence post stands behind him at the left; the top of the fence runs straight across the picture high up, and above it is a narrow band of evening sky. At the right edge of the frame, close to us and cut off by the edge, a sliver of one party guest watching, back to us, a cup in one hand.

COMPOSITION. Follow image 1 exactly: the same camera, the same perspective, the same position and size of the spinner, the same two rings (the left one seen nearly edge-on as a tall narrow ellipse, the right one rounder), the same two flames (one low at the left at the height of his hand, one high at the upper right), the two thin chains from his hands to the flames, the fence top, the fence post, the line where the fence meets the lawn. Image 1 is only a layout drawing from a 3D model: replace its mannequin with the real man and its ovals with real flames, and do not keep its mechanical even line. His hands are real hands closed round the handles, five fingers each.

WHAT HE IS DOING. He is in the middle of the pattern, quite calm, his weight low, his head tipped back to follow the upper flame, so that we see his throat and the underside of his jaw and nothing of his face.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The fire is the light, and FIRE IS BARE PAPER. Image 2 shows it: its white shapes are the two rings, the two flames and the patch of lawn the fire lights. Each ring is a ribbon of clean bare cream paper, NOT PAINTED, about as wide as a finger, edged along both sides with a thin line of transparent orange wash that feathers a little outward, like the edge of a wet brushstroke: the ribbon is narrow and a little broken and tapering behind each flame, as a real trail of fire is, not a perfect hoop of wire. Each flame is a small teardrop of bare paper with the same thin orange edge and a touch of deeper orange-red at its tail. That orange is the only colour in the whole picture. No yellow fill, no white paint, no glow, no halo, no rays, no haze, no bloom, no sparks, no smoke: the fire is simply where the brush did not go, with an orange edge.
The grass under and round his feet, where the firelight falls, is also bare cream paper, with only a few thin ink strokes for grass blades. Everything else is under one even transparent dusk-violet ink wash: the fence deeper, the lawn further off a little paler, the band of sky above the fence the palest violet of all. The spinner himself is line under that violet wash, with no local colour, a little bare paper left along the edges of his arms and shins that face the flames. The edge between wash and bare paper is a clean, slightly soft wet edge. No black anywhere.

THE TWO RINGS OF FIRE are the one thing being looked at: they carry the finest, most careful line in the picture, each a complete, true ellipse with a thin margin of clean paper round it.
