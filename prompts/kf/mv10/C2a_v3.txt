A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A living room in Mountain View, California, 2010, late in a housewarming party, night outside, the lamps on. Two people stand close together, facing each other, in front of a wall of bookshelves. We look over the shoulder of a tall young man who stands in the left foreground with his back to us, large: the back of his head, his nape, his shoulders and upper back fill the left third of the frame. His head is tipped forward and down: he is looking down at something low between them, out of frame, where her hand lies on his forearm. Facing him, right of centre, a little shorter than he is, stands a 27-year-old Chinese woman, the host, seen from the chest up in three-quarter view, her face turned toward him and up to him. Behind them the wall is all books: a dark-wood bookcase filled edge to edge with uniform sets of thick reference volumes on four shelves; right behind her head the upright where it meets a second bookcase; and beside her head a short run of small slim paperbacks. No book has a title: every spine is blank. A third person stands at the far RIGHT edge of the frame, nearer to us than she is: her childhood friend, seen from behind in three-quarter back view, cut off by the right edge and the bottom edge, so that we see only the back of his head, one ear, his left shoulder and upper back. He has turned his head a little toward the two of them and is keeping quite still.

The looping, wiry outlines round the two heads in image 1 are the outlines of soft shadows on the books (see LIGHT): paint them as shadows, not as lines. Her arm on the left of the picture reaches forward and down toward his forearm and leaves the frame at the bottom; no hands need show.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She suddenly wants to tell him something else, something that matters more than the bookshelf, and her voice will not work. Her mouth has opened a little to say it and stopped: the lips parted about a finger's width, soft, not shaped round any word, the edge of the upper teeth just showing, the jaw still: a breath taken in and held. No sound is coming. Her eyes are on his face, both irises turned up and toward the left of the picture, to where his eyes are; they are very bright and visibly WET: a thin clear line of water stands along each lower lid, drawn as a fine line of bare paper just above the lower lashes, and has not fallen; the rims of the lids are a little pink, the lower lashes damp and clumped, the whites faintly pink at the corners. No tear on the cheek. She has just blinked it back and will have to again. Her brows are resting, soft, only the faintest lift at their inner ends; her upper lids are a little lowered, as lids are just after a blink. This is tenderness, not alarm: she is NOT startled, NOT frightened, NOT surprised, NOT worried; the eyes are not widened. She is moved, and very fond of him, and it has caught in her throat. Each eye glistens: besides the catch-light there is a second, smaller glint low in the eye where the water stands. Her head is perfectly still. She is not smiling and not crying: she is holding still so that it stays in.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. Three paper floor lamps stand behind us and light the two of them and the fronts of the books; everything the lamps do not reach is the night. Image 2 shows it: pale = lamp light, darker = shade. The lamp-lit book spines are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines for each book. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. The lamps throw soft overlapping shadows of the two heads and shoulders onto the books behind and to the left of them, as in image 2: each shadow is one even transparent dusk-violet ink wash laid across the bare-paper books, the outlines showing through, with a clean, slightly soft wet edge and no outline; where shadows overlap the wash is one step deeper. The dark-wood shelves and uprights and the gaps above the books are under the same dusk-violet wash, a little deeper. No black anywhere. Her face is in the lamp light and is painted normally with no wash over it: warm rosy flesh colour, the pink blush on her cheeks clear and fresh, never grey, never ashen. In each eye one crisp catch-light of bare paper, a soft upright oblong like a paper lamp shade, a little larger than usual because the eyes are wet, on the same side in both eyes.

THE ONE THING IN FULL COLOUR is her face: it carries the finest, most careful line in the picture. She is drawn quietly, thin line and pale wash, nothing emphasised. The books stay line on bare paper or line under violet wash, with no local colour of their own.
