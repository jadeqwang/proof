SUBJECT. A close portrait. A 27-year-old Chinese woman stands with her back to her own bookshelves at her housewarming party in Mountain View, California, 2010, at dusk with the lamps on, and sings one word straight to us. She is seen frontally, level with her eyes, from very near: her head and shoulders fill the middle of the frame, cut off at the chest by the bottom edge. Behind her, out of focus in the drawing only by being simpler: three shelves of a dark-wood bookcase filled with uniform thick reference volumes; on the middle shelf, just left of her head, a short run of small slim paperbacks; at the far right the upright where the dark bookcase ends and a sliver of a second, pale bookcase with a few leaning books. No book has a title: every spine is blank.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She is singing the word "proof" and holding its vowel. Her head is perfectly still and squared to us, chin level. Her lips are pushed forward and rounded into a small "oo": a small, soft, round opening, the lips full and gathered, a little dark inside, no teeth showing, the cheeks slightly drawn in. The mouth shape is clean, simple and easy to read. Both irises point exactly into the lens and stay there: steady, wide open, a little shiny, waiting to see whether we follow her. Her eyebrows are lifted a little at their inner ends, the way a person's are when she has said the truest thing she has and cannot back it up. She is not smiling. Her shoulders are level and still.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. Three paper floor lamps stand behind us and light her face and the fronts of the books; everything the lamps do not reach is the dusk. Image 2 shows it: pale = lamp light, darker = shade. The lamp-lit book spines are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines for each book. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. The lamps throw one large soft shadow of her own head and shoulder onto the books at the upper left, beside her, as in image 2: one even transparent dusk-violet ink wash laid across the bare-paper books, the outlines showing through, with a clean, slightly soft wet edge. The dark-wood shelves and uprights and the gaps above the books are under the same dusk-violet wash, a little deeper. No black anywhere. She stands in the lamp light, so her face is painted normally with no wash over it: warm rosy flesh colour, the pink blush on her cheeks clear and fresh, never grey, never ashen. In each eye one small crisp catch-light of bare paper, a soft upright oblong like a paper lamp shade, on the same side in both eyes.

THE ONE THING IN FULL COLOUR is her face: it carries the finest, most careful line in the picture. The books stay line on bare paper or line under violet wash, with no local colour of their own.
