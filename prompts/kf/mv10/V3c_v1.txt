A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A housewarming party in Mountain View, California, 2010, at dusk. We stand on the patio in the back yard and look at the back of the house: a plain wall with a wide sliding glass door, its left leaf slid open. The host, a 27-year-old Chinese woman, stands in the open doorway facing us, greeting a guest who has just come up to her. That guest stands in the left foreground with his back to us, large, seen from the waist up: he is a friend of a friend, and he is asking her for something, earnestly. Behind her, through the open door, the living room: one paper floor lamp with a plain cylindrical shade just inside the door at the left, a small framed picture on the far wall, a hall doorway, and three or four guests standing about with cups, small and far. At the right of the frame, behind the fixed glass leaf of the sliding door and in front of it, two more guests stand close together, seen from the side and behind, cut by the right edge.

COMPOSITION. Follow image 1 exactly: the same camera, the same perspective, the same positions and sizes of the guest's back and head at the left, his raised arm cut by the top left corner, the door frame (the top rail, the upright in the middle of the picture, the upright at the right), the woman in the doorway with one arm raised high, the lamp, the small figures inside, the two guests at the right edge. Image 1 is only a layout drawing from a 3D model: replace its mannequins with the real people and its boxes with real things (the boxes low inside the room are a sofa and a side table), and do not keep its mechanical even line.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She is the host and she is playing the part of a bright, welcoming American girl, and playing it very well. Her arm on the left of the picture is thrown straight up above her head in a wave, hand open, fingers spread, five fingers: a wave a little too big for a guest standing two steps away, and started a moment too soon. Her other hand holds out a tall glass of lemonade toward him at waist height. Her head is tipped a few degrees to one side, her eyebrows are up, her eyes are wide and fixed exactly on his face, her mouth is open on a bright sung-out "hi", the corners lifted: an expression made carefully and accurately and turned up about fifteen per cent further than the moment needs. Her back is straight and she is up on the balls of her feet. Not a caricature, not mocking: a part played well.
The guest in the foreground leans in toward her, his head pushed a little forward, one hand raised beside his head (at the top left corner) with the index finger up, the way someone raises a point he has prepared. He stands at ordinary strength: ordinary posture, ordinary line.

SHE IS DRAWN LOUDER THAN EVERYONE ELSE, and this is the point of the picture. She alone is drawn with a slightly heavier, darker ink outline (about fifteen per cent thicker than every other line in the picture) and with washes one step richer: a deep indigo navy dress, a bright orange belt, blacker hair, warm rosy skin, a little more colour in cheeks and lips. Image 3 shows this: its LEFT figure is the ordinary drawing, its RIGHT figure is how she is drawn here. She and her glass are the only things in the picture in full local colour; the lemonade is a clear pale lemon yellow with one round slice of lemon in it. A thin margin of clean bare paper round her whole figure, so that she seems lit from within. Everyone else is thin, pale, even line with no colour of their own.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The sun is very low behind us. The yard fence shades the patio and the lower house wall; the last sun passes over it and goes in through the open door. Image 2 shows it. The open doorway is the light: the room seen through it, its floor, walls, lamp shade and far wall, is NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines for the lamp, the picture, the furniture and the small guests inside. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. She stands in that light and is painted normally, with no wash over her. Everything outside the doorway, the house wall, the door frame, the fixed glass at the right with the guests there, the patio, the foreground guest's back, is under one even transparent dusk-violet ink wash, a little deeper at the bottom, the ink lines showing through, with no other colour in it. The edge between wash and bare paper is a clean, slightly soft wet edge. No black anywhere. In each of her eyes one small crisp catch-light of bare paper, a low horizontal sliver like the last of the sun, on the same side in both eyes.

PEOPLE. Jade, 2010, age 27: a Chinese woman; an oval face with a softly pointed chin, almond eyes with deep near-black irises (almost as dark as her hair), dark, fairly strong, slightly asymmetric eyebrows, a straight nose, full lips. Thin dark rectangular half-rim glasses. Long black hair with NO fringe: an open forehead, parted slightly off-centre, swept to both sides in long soft face-framing layers with a loose wave, past the shoulders. A dark navy jersey dress to just above the knee with a scoop neck and three-quarter sleeves, a narrow orange belt at the waist, a thin chain with one small round pale-blue pendant, black opaque tights. No logos, no lettering, no pattern. Image 4 is her accepted face sheet: reproduce THAT woman's face, glasses, hair and dress exactly; keep her natural, real face.
The guests (image 5 is their costume sheet; use it for clothes only, all under the violet wash here, without the sheet's colours). The foreground guest: a man in a plain T-shirt with a messenger-bag strap across his back, a baseball cap worn backwards; we see only the back of his head, his neck, his back and his raised hand. The two at the right edge: a tall thin man in a zip hoodie and a woman with one long braid in a fleece vest, each with a plain cup. NO GUEST HAS A FACE: wherever a guest's head is turned enough to show a face, it is a simple shape under the same wash as the wall behind it, with the hairline, ear and jaw in thin line and no features, like a figure the painter has not reached yet. Not a mask, not a white oval, not a blur. Only the host has eyes, a nose and a mouth.
