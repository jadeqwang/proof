A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. Inside a wall, in the dark: a five-year-old Chinese girl is hiding in the narrow space between two walls of a building in the Netherlands, winter 1988, holding completely still and silent. We are level with her eyes and very close: her face fills the frame, from the fringe at the top edge to the chin at the bottom edge, facing us squarely. Nothing else is in the picture: no room, no furniture, no other person, only the dark and her, and one thin strip of light.

It touches a few strands of hair at her temple at the upper left, crosses the eye on the LEFT of the picture and the bridge of her nose, ends on her cheek beside the nose, and shows once more as a short bar at the lower right corner, beyond her jaw, on the rough brick behind her. WHAT SHE IS DOING. She is watching one tiny thing floating in the strip of light, a hand's width in front of her face. Her head is perfectly still. The eye in the light is wide open, bright and fixed, its dark iris almost in the middle of the eye, turned only a very little toward her nose and slightly down, resting on that near point, the way a child looks when she is counting something: wide with attention, not with fright, and not cross-eyed. Her brows are resting. Her lips are together and level, her jaw loose; the mouth is in the dark. She is not smiling and not frowning and not posing for anyone: all the life in the face is in that one eye. Do not draw the thing she is watching.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. Image 2 shows where the light is: only the thin diagonal strip (its white shapes). The strip is NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines of what it crosses. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. Its two edges are straight and parallel, each a clean, slightly soft wet edge. Everything else in the picture, nine tenths of it, is dark: a near-black indigo ink wash, laid in several transparent layers, soft and deep, with the paper grain and fibres still breathing through it and slow, cloudy variations in its depth; never flat black, never a solid fill. Her face outside the strip is almost as dark as the wall behind it, only one step lighter: a viewer finds it a moment after finding the eye. In that dark the rest of her is barely there, given only by a slightly paler edge or a slightly darker line: the curve of one cheek, the two brows, the nostrils, the closed line of the mouth, a few strands of hair. The OTHER eye, on the right of the picture, is nearly lost: a soft dark almond with the faint line of its upper lid and lashes, no white showing, no highlight, no catch-light, so that it cannot be seen where it is looking. The dark is a deep indigo-blue ink, cool, not brown; the little that shows of her skin in it is still warm under the glaze, not grey and not ashen.

THE ONE THING IN FULL DEPTH: the eye in the strip of light. It is the only thing in the picture painted in full colour and with the finest, most loving line: every upper lash drawn, the lid's fold, the wet lower rim, a deep brown-black iris with its darker ring and pupil, one small crisp catch-light of bare paper in it shaped like a short thin slit (the crack the light comes through), and on the bare-paper skin around it a breath of warm rosy flesh colour. Nothing else in the frame has any colour of its own.

THE HANDS, kept very quiet. At the very bottom edge of the frame, at the lower right where the strip of light leaves the picture, and cut off by the bottom edge: the knuckles of an adult woman's hand closed round the child's small hand, holding it. Only the four knuckles and the backs of the woman's fingers, with two or three of the child's small fingertips showing from inside her grip. The strip of light just catches the knuckles: where it crosses them they are bare paper with thin ink outlines, and the rest of both hands is lost in the same dark wash. The woman's hand is strong and capable, short plain nails, no rings; image 7 shows her hands (for the hand's shape and the line only). No sleeve, no arm, no second person's face or body anywhere: only the knuckles. They are small in the frame and easy to miss.
