A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A five-year-old Chinese girl sits cross-legged on the bare floorboards of a dusty attic above a restaurant in the Netherlands on a winter morning in 1988, facing us, seen level with her eyes from less than a metre away, head and body centred in the frame, cut off at the knees by the bottom edge. Behind her: the sloping underside of the roof with exposed rafters at the upper left, a plain plastered wall, wide old floorboards running back, and on the floor at the left one taped cardboard moving box with a folded quilt lying on top of it. Nothing else.

COMPOSITION. Image 1 is an earlier take of this exact painting. Repaint it as it is: the same camera, the same child in the same place at the same size, the same face, the same eyes looking the same way, the same hands on the knees, the same rafters, wall and floorboards, the same light and the same wash. Image 2 is the layout drawing it was made from (a 3D model, composition only; do not keep its mechanical even line). Change only two things. ONE: the plain cube on the floor at the left becomes a real thing, in the same place and the same size: a used cardboard moving box, its flaps shut and taped across the top with one strip of brown paper tape, its corners a little crushed, and lying on top of it a folded quilt, two folds thick, its quilting stitches drawn in thin line; box and quilt are in the shade, line under the same wash, with no colour of their own. TWO: the attic is dusty and old: a soft grey film of dust lies on the top of the box, along the tops of the rafters and in the corner where the floor meets the wall; the floorboards are dry and scuffed, with knots, a few gaps and nail heads; one small cobweb hangs in the angle between a rafter and the roof. The dust lies on things; there is none in the air.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She has just looked up from a paper keyboard on the floor in front of her. Her hands rest on her knees. She is watching one single speck of dust drift through the air. Her head is perfectly still and faces us squarely; only her eyes have moved: both irises are turned toward the right side of the picture and very slightly upward, converged on a point an arm's length in front of her, fixed and bright, the way a child looks when she is counting something. Her lips are together and level, her jaw loose, her brows resting. She is not smiling and not frowning and not posing for anyone: all the life in the face is in the eyes. Do not draw the speck of dust.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. As in image 1, the low winter sunlight falls only on her lap, her hands and knees and the strip of floor at the very bottom of the frame. Those sunlit parts are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. Everything above that, her face, hair, shoulders and the whole room, is in shade: one even transparent cool blue-grey ink wash lies over it, a little darker under the roof. On her face the wash is a thin glaze laid OVER a normally painted face: her warm rosy flesh colour and the pink blush on her cheeks still show clearly through it, never grey, never ashen. In each eye there is one small crisp catch-light of bare paper, squarish like a distant window, on the same side in both eyes. The line between shade and sun crosses her body just above the lap as a clean, slightly soft wet edge.
