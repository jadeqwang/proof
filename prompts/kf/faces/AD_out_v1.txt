SUBJECT. WHAT SHE IS DOING. She has just looked up from a paper keyboard on the floor in front of her. Her hands rest on her knees. She is watching one single speck of dust drift through the air. Her head is perfectly still and faces us squarely; only her eyes have moved: both irises are turned toward the right side of the picture and very slightly upward, converged on a point an arm's length in front of her, fixed and bright, the way a child looks when she is counting something. Her lips are together and level, her jaw loose, her brows resting. She is not smiling and not frowning and not posing for anyone: all the life in the face is in the eyes. Do not draw the speck of dust.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. Image 2 shows where the low winter sunlight falls: only on her lap, her hands and knees and the strip of floor at the very bottom of the frame (its white shapes). Everything above that, her face, hair, shoulders and the whole room, is in shade: one even transparent cool blue-grey ink wash lies over it, a little darker under the roof. On her face the wash is a thin glaze laid OVER a normally painted face: her warm rosy flesh colour and the pink blush on her cheeks still show clearly through it, never grey, never ashen. In each eye there is one small crisp catch-light of bare paper, squarish like a distant window, on the same side in both eyes. The line between shade and sun crosses her body just above the lap as a clean, slightly soft wet edge.
