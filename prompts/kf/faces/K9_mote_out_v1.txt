A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A kitchen in Decatur, Illinois, 1990, a bright morning. A seven-year-old Chinese girl stands alone, facing us, seen level with her eyes from a little over a metre away: head, shoulders and chest, in the middle of the frame. Her arms hang loose at her sides. Behind her there is only a plain kitchen wall. Nothing else: no furniture, no other person.

COMPOSITION. Follow image 1 exactly: the same camera, her head in the middle of the frame at the same size with the eyes two fifths of the way down, her shoulders and upper arms leaving the frame at the bottom, the wide empty wall on both sides of her. Image 1 is only a layout drawing from a 3D model: replace its mannequin with the real child (the slab across the top of the head is her fringe), and do not keep its mechanical even line. Leave the empty wall empty.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. Nobody is asking anything of her now, and she can think again. She is watching one tiny thing floating in the sunlight an arm's length in front of her. Her head is perfectly still. Her lips are together and level, her jaw loose, her brows resting. She is not smiling and not frowning and not posing for anyone: all the life in the face is in the eyes, which are fixed and bright on that one point, the way a child looks when she is counting. Her head faces us squarely; only her eyes have moved: both irises are turned a little toward the left side of the picture and very slightly upward, converged on that near point. Her shoulders are down and easy. Do not draw the thing she is watching. This is the quietest picture in the film: nothing in it moves, and nothing in it is asking to be looked at except her eyes.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. One shaft of morning sun from a window behind us falls on her (image 2 shows it reaching only to her collar: in the painting it reaches higher, to the top of her fringe). Her whole face, her neck, her sweatshirt and her arms are in the sun, and they are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. On her sunlit face the skin is bare paper: the features are drawn in the finest ink line of the picture, with only a soft warm pink blush on the cheeks, a soft rose on the lips, and dark irises each holding one small crisp catch-light of bare paper, upright and oblong like a window, on the same side in both eyes. Her hair is glossy black ink. The upper edge of the shaft crosses her hair just above the fringe, slanting gently down to the right, as a clean, slightly soft wet edge; the crown of her head above it and the whole wall behind her are in shade: one even transparent warm umber ink wash, the ink lines showing through it, nothing in it darker than a mid brown, no black, quite plain, a little deeper toward the top corners. Her sunlit figure stands against that wash as the one unpainted shape in the picture.

THE GIRL. Little Jade at about age 7, Decatur, Illinois, 1990: a Chinese girl in first or second grade, with LARGE round dark eyes set wide apart, each with thick dark upper lashes; distinct, fairly strong, straight dark eyebrows; a tiny button nose; a small soft mouth; soft cheeks with a wide warm pink blush. Proportions of a seven-year-old: about five and a half heads tall, longer and thinner arms and legs than a small child, a round face with the chin a touch longer. Glossy jet-black hair (pure black ink, never brown): a light fringe of a few soft parted strands with the forehead showing between them, long side locks in front of the ears, a small half-ponytail tied high at the back of the crown with a dark band, the rest straight down to her shoulder blades. An oversized plain heather-grey crew-neck sweatshirt with the sleeves pushed up to the elbows, no print and no lettering; dark navy stirrup leggings; bare feet.
