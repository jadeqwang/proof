A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A kitchen in Decatur, Illinois, 1990, a bright morning. A Chinese woman of about 38 walks past a tall china cabinet with glazed doors, on her way to the hall doorway at the right. We see her from behind and a little to her left, from the chest up, in back three-quarter view at the right of the picture: her permed black curls, one ear with the wire arm of her glasses over it, the line of her cheek and jaw, her shoulder in a cardigan. In the dark glass of the cabinet door beside her, filling the left and middle of the picture, is her own reflection: her face in three-quarter view, the same size as her real head, with the cabinet's shelves and a few plain bowls and cups seen faintly through it. She is looking ahead at the doorway. She does not look at the glass and does not know the reflection is there. At the far right edge, the upright frame of the hall doorway.

THE REFLECTED FACE: the one thing drawn with the most care. In the glass her face is at rest. Her lips are together and level, her jaw loose, her brows resting; her eyes look straight ahead at the doorway she is walking to, steady and exact, like a swimmer on the blocks watching the water. She is not smiling and not frowning and not posing for anyone: all the life in the face is in the eyes. It is the same still face her small daughter has, and she has never seen it. The reflection is drawn in the same fine ink line as a real face, complete (both eyes behind the round wire glasses, the dark straight brows, the nose, the mouth, the curls ending at the jaw), but paler and thinner than she is, a face in a dark window: the warm rosy colour of her skin shows faintly through the glass, and the rims of two bowls on a shelf cross it as thin lines.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The sun shaft of this room is behind us and low: it falls only on the lowest part of the picture, across her cardigan below the shoulder blades and the bottom rail of the cabinet door, and that band is NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. Its upper edge slants gently down to the right and is a clean, slightly soft wet edge. Everything above it, the woman's head and shoulders, the cabinet, the glass and the door frame, is in shade: one even transparent warm umber ink wash, the ink lines showing through it, nothing in it darker than a mid brown, no black; the glass of the cabinet doors is the deepest part of that wash, a clear dark brown in which the reflection floats, never black. On her reflected face the wash is a thin glaze laid OVER a normally painted face: warm and rosy underneath, never grey, never ashen. In each reflected eye one small crisp catch-light of bare paper, upright and oblong like a window.
