WHAT SHE IS DOING. A grown-up has just told her that her face has no expression and that she should try to have some, right now. So she makes one, on purpose, with great care. ONE eye, the eye on the LEFT of the picture, is narrowed nearly shut, the lower lid pushed up; the other eye stays wide open. Both brows are pulled down and together into a furrow, with a small knot between them. Her lips are pressed together in a straight line, and she lets out a small sigh through her nose: the nostrils a little widened, the chest and shoulders just sinking. It is a five-year-old's honest idea of what having an expression is: a little too much, completely sincere, not mocking anyone and not cross with anyone. It is funny because she means it. Her open eye looks straight out at the grown-up, who is where we are, just above the lens.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. As in image 1, the low winter sunlight falls only on her lap, her hands and knees and the strip of floor at the very bottom of the frame. Those sunlit parts are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. Everything above that, her face, hair, shoulders and the whole room are in shade: one even transparent cool blue-grey ink wash lies over it all, a little darker under the roof. On her face the wash is a thin glaze laid OVER a normally painted face: her warm rosy flesh colour and the pink blush on her cheeks still show clearly through it, never grey, never ashen. In each eye there is one small crisp catch-light of bare paper, squarish like a distant window, on the same side in both eyes. The catch-light is in the open eye, and a sliver of it shows in the narrowed one. The line between shade and sun crosses her body just above the lap as a clean, slightly soft wet edge.
