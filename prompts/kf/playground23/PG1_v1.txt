A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. An indoor, space-themed children's playground in Austin, Texas, in 2023, late in the afternoon, almost empty. A wide view of the room from standing height, looking straight at its far wall. The right two thirds of the far wall are one big glass wall, floor to ceiling: tall panes of plate glass in slim dark aluminium mullions, one thin rail near the top, a low sill. The left third of the far wall is solid, and painted on it is part of the playground's space mural: the planets as plain circles of different sizes drawn in thin line, one of them with a simple ring, one a crescent, scattered across the wall, and among them one small pale blue dot. The floor is padded foam tiles with jigsaw joins. In the room: at the far left, in front of the mural, a small rocket-shaped climbing tower with round portholes and a short slide; near it three big soft foam blocks and a round floor cushion. A low sun comes in through the glass. In front of the glass, to the right of centre, a grandfather of about seventy and his grandson of about four stand side by side, hand in hand, both in pure profile facing the left of the picture, seen whole from head to foot. In the near foreground at the lower left, seen from behind and cut off at the hips by the bottom edge, stands the woman who is looking at them: the boy's mother, the old man's daughter, aged 40. Her face is reflected faintly in the glass, to the left of the two figures. At the left edge of the floor, a toddler runs past: only a small blur.

COMPOSITION. Image 1 is a closer view of this same moment, painted earlier: this picture is the same glass wall, the same light, the same two figures and the same reflection seen from several metres further back, so that the whole room is in view. Keep the look of image 1's glass, mullions, sill and floor tiles, the look and the poses of its two silhouettes, and the look of its reflection. Here the grandfather is about one third of the picture's height, standing on the floor in front of the glass at about two thirds of the way across the picture; the boy is on his left, reaching only a little above the old man's waist, his raised hand in the old man's lowered hand. The woman's back is large in the lower left foreground: the back of her head, her long hair, her shoulders and her back, turned a little to the right, toward the two figures. Her reflection is small: a head and shoulders in the upper part of the glass between her and the two figures, facing us. The space between the woman and the two figures is open floor and light: nothing stands in it.

THE TWO FIGURES AGAINST THE LIGHT. They are seen against the sunlit glass, so each of them is one plain shape of deeper warm grey-gold wash, a soft silhouette with a clean brushed edge, noticeably darker than the wash round it but never black. Inside each shape there is nothing: no eye, no mouth, no nostril, no ear, no hair strands, no folds or buttons of clothing, no modelling. They are not portraits of anyone. Only the outline says who they are: the old man a little stooped, slight, with thin short hair, a shirt collar, a loose cardigan and trousers; the boy with a big round head, a tuft of soft hair at the crown, a T-shirt, shorts and a child's short arms and legs. The outlines must show that the two are the same: they stand in the same way, perfectly still, and both heads are tipped forward and a little down by exactly the same few degrees, looking at the same spot ahead of them, absorbed, unaware of being watched. Tall and small, the same figure twice.

THE WOMAN AND HER REFLECTION. In the foreground she stands quite still with her arms at her sides, her head turned toward the two figures; we do not see her face there, only the back of her head and the edge of her cheek and glasses. Her reflection in the glass is the same woman facing us: the same hair, the same glasses, the same cardigan. It is drawn in thin ink line only: no wash of its own and no colour, the wash of the glass showing straight through it, as if drawn on the glass with the very point of the brush. Faint, but exact. In it her head is perfectly still, her lips together and level, her jaw loose, her brows resting. She is not smiling and not frowning and not posing for anyone: all the life in the face is in the eyes. It is the same face as the two silhouettes, the same stillness and the same small forward tilt of the head: she is seeing her own face from outside for the first time.

THE TODDLER. Her small daughter, running: at the far left edge of the floor, a little figure no taller than the foam blocks, painted as a soft moving blur, three or four wet strokes of the same wash with no outline and no features, leaning into her run.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The low sun comes through the glass wall and crosses the room as ONE broad, straight diagonal band, from the top of the glass at the upper right down to the floor at the lower left, at about 40 degrees. The band, and the part of the glass and the floor it lies on, are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only the thin ink outlines of mullions, sill and floor tiles inside it. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom, no lens flare, no sun disc: light is simply where the brush did not go. The two figures stand in front of the band, dark against it. Everything outside the band, the rest of the glass, the mural wall, the ceiling, the rocket tower, the foam blocks, the rest of the floor and the woman in the foreground, is under one even transparent warm grey-gold ink wash, quiet, a little deeper in the near corners and over the woman's back, with every line showing through it and no local colour: her hair and clothes are line under that wash. The band has two straight edges, and the edge between wash and blank paper is a clean, slightly soft wet edge.

THE ONE SPOT OF COLOUR. The small pale blue dot in the mural is the only local colour in the whole picture: a plain round dot of pale blue no bigger than the boy's hand, with a hair of clean paper round it. Every other planet is an empty circle in thin line under the wash. The mural has no lettering, no faces, no rockets, no stars with points.
