A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. The photograph Voyager 1 took in 1990 looking back at the Earth from the edge of the solar system, the "pale blue dot" frame, repainted in this hand as ink wash on paper. There is no object in the picture at all: only empty space and stray sunlight. Almost the whole sheet is one near-black indigo ink wash, soft, slightly uneven, the paper grain showing through it, never flat black. Across that wash run four straight, parallel, diagonal bands of scattered sunlight, each crossing the whole sheet from the upper right to the lower left.

COMPOSITION. Image 1 is only a guide for the ANGLE: the straight diagonal edge between its black and its white areas has exactly the slope every band must have. Do not copy its shapes, only the slope: all four bands are parallel to that edge, rising from the lower left to the upper right at about 35 degrees from the horizontal. One broad band passes through the centre of the picture, about one seventh of the picture's height wide. Above and to the left of it, well separated, one narrower band. Below and to the right of it, two thin bands, fainter. The bands have straight sides and run right off both edges of the sheet; they do not fan out, they do not converge, there is no source.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The bands are NOT PAINTED. The broad central band is bare cream paper left blank. The three others are paper under only the thinnest veil of the same indigo wash, so that they are fainter than the central one. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom, no lens flare, no rainbow colour in the bands: light is simply where the brush did not go. The edge of each band is a clean, slightly soft wet edge. The wash between the bands is even and quiet, a little deeper toward the corners.

THE ONE THING. Inside the broad central band, a little to the right of the centre of the picture, there is one single tiny pale blue dot: a pinprick, the smallest mark the point of a fine brush can make, far smaller than a grain of rice, almost too small to find. It is pale blue. It is the only colour in the whole picture and the only mark inside any band.
