A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A pale laminate meeting table in a school counselling office, December, about 1991, seen through the eyes of a child of eight who sits at it, leaning in close over the sheet of paper in front of her. The sheet lies in the left half of the frame: light canary-yellow US Letter photocopy paper, portrait way up. On it is a grid of small EMPTY circles drawn in a thin, even, pale grey line: FIVE rows. Rows one, two, three and five have exactly SEVEN circles each. Row four has exactly SIX circles, and it is centred on the page, so its six circles sit half a step in from both ends of the other rows, each one under the space between two circles of the row above. Count the rows before finishing: 7, 7, 7, 6, 7. Every circle is the same size and is EMPTY: plain yellow inside, no face, no dot, no mark. There is NOTHING else on the sheet: no title, no heading bar, no bars or lines under the circles, no words, no speckle, no border. The child's own right hand comes in from the lower right corner of the frame and POINTS: the right hand and bare forearm of a Chinese girl of about eight: a small, slim child's hand, soft, with short clean nails, exactly five fingers; the sleeve of an oversized plain heather-grey sweatshirt is pushed up toward the elbow and shows at the bottom edge of the frame. Her index finger is stretched out straight and its tip rests on the paper at the LAST circle of the short fourth row, the sixth circle, the one at the right-hand end of that row, where the row stops one short. The other three fingers are curled under and the thumb is tucked against them. The right half of the frame is empty table.
