A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. The top of a pale laminate meeting table in a school counselling office, December, about 1991, seen from close above and to one side, from the place of a child sitting at the table. In the middle of the frame a man's open left hand lies on the table, palm up. His forearm comes in from the upper left of the frame, in the sleeve of a pale-blue cotton shirt with the cuff buttoned, and a plain steel wristwatch on that wrist. The hand is offered, not asking: the back of the hand rests on the table, the palm is open to the ceiling, the four fingers lie together, long and relaxed and very slightly curled, pointing to the right, the thumb lying a little apart. It is the hand of a slim Chinese man of 38 who types all day: long fingers, neat short nails, clear knuckles, the creases of the palm drawn. Behind the hand, at the top of the frame, a round pencil cup stands on the table with a few yellow pencils in it. At the right edge, the corner of a clipboard with a sheet of lined notepaper (pale ruled lines only, nothing written), and above it, at the top right corner, the fingers of another person's hand resting on the table holding a pencil. At the left, the edge of the table and, beyond and below it, the carpet. No faces anywhere: every person is cut off by the frame.
