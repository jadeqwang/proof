A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A school counselling office, December, about 1991, seen from close by, from the place of a child sitting at a meeting table and looking sideways at the hands of her mother, who sits beside her. In the middle of the frame, just above the edge of the pale laminate table, a woman's two hands hold a small, thick pocket dictionary: a Chinese-English dictionary no bigger than her palm, with a plain red cloth cover, soft worn corners and NO lettering on it. Her left hand cradles the book from underneath, fingers wrapped round its spine and back cover. The thumb of her right hand is racing through the pages: the pad of the thumb is bent against the fore-edge and the thin pages are springing off it one after another, fanned out in the air in a blur of many fine close lines, some pages caught in mid-flip. Her right forearm comes up from the bottom of the frame, her left from the left: the sleeves of a plain cream cotton blouse pushed up above the elbows. They are the strong capable hands of a Chinese woman of 38, a former swimmer: short plain nails, no rings, no jewellery. Her body is a large soft shape at the left edge, cut off by the frame. The table top runs away to the right. Beyond the hands, across the corner of the table and out of the light, the shirt front and resting forearm of a man sitting at the end of the table, cut off at the chest by the top of the frame, and the back of a wooden chair. No faces anywhere: every person is cut off by the frame.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. One fluorescent tube is overhead; its light falls straight down. Image 2 shows it: the table top at the right, the upper faces of her hands and wrists and the fanned edges of the pages catch it; the undersides of her arms, her body and the man beyond are in shade. The lit table top and the lit edges of the flying pages are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. No white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. Everything else is under one even transparent greenish-grey ink wash, flat and quiet, with thin ink lines showing through it and no black anywhere. The edge between shade and light is a clean, slightly soft wet edge.

THE ONE THING IN THIS PICTURE: the little red dictionary and the thumb on it. The cover is the only strong colour in the picture: a worn cinnabar red cloth, a little faded at the corners and the spine, the weave of the cloth showing. The pages are bare paper with the finest grey lines: many, thin, exact, crowded where they leave the thumb and spreading as they fall, the page edges slightly yellowed. On the open pages no words can be read: only the faintest pale grey horizontal hairlines where columns of tiny print would be. Her hands are in a pale warm rosy flesh wash, drawn with the same fine and loving line: the bent thumb, its nail, the knuckles. A thin margin of clean bare paper round the book and hands so that they seem lit from within. Everything else stays line under wash with no local colour of its own.
