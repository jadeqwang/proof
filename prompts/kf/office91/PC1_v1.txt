A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A small counselling office in an American elementary school, December, about 1991. A Chinese girl of about eight sits at a meeting table facing us, right of centre in the frame, seen level with her eyes from about two metres, cut off at the chest by the table edge along the bottom of the frame. Her forearms lie on the table and her two hands rest palm down at the bottom edge, one either side of a sheet of paper that lies on the table just below the frame (we do not see the paper). She sits on a plain wooden school chair whose back shows behind her shoulders. Behind her is a bare painted wall with nothing on it: the whole left half of the frame is empty quiet wall. At the far right edge, the tall narrow side of a grey steel filing cabinet. Nothing else.

COMPOSITION. Follow image 1 exactly: the same camera, the same size and position of her head and body (her face right of centre, the left half of the frame empty wall), the same chair back, table edge and cabinet edge. WHAT SHE IS DOING. She is reading down the page on the table in front of her, checking it row by row. Her head is tipped a little down and perfectly still. Both eyes are lowered to the page: we see her upper eyelids and thick lashes and, under them, both dark irises pointing down and very slightly toward the left of the picture, fixed on one exact spot low on the page, the way a child looks when she is counting. Her lips are together and level, her jaw loose, her brows resting. She is not smiling and not frowning and not posing for anyone: all the life in the face is in the eyes.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. One fluorescent tube is overhead, out of frame; its light falls straight down in a narrow pool over the table and the child, and does not reach the wall behind her. Image 2 shows it: the table top, her hands and forearms and the tops of her shoulders are the lightest things. The table top, the tops of her forearms and hands are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. No white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. The wall, the chair, the cabinet edge and everything else are under one even transparent greenish-grey ink wash, flat and quiet, the same all over, with thin ink lines showing through it and no black anywhere. The child is under the tube: the top of her glossy black hair carries a soft sheen, her shoulders and sweatshirt are thin line with the palest grey. Her face is tipped down away from the tube, so on her face the wash is a thin glaze laid OVER a normally painted face: her warm rosy flesh colour and the pink blush on her cheeks still show clearly through it, never grey, never ashen. In each eye one small crisp catch-light of bare paper, a short thin horizontal bar like the reflection of a fluorescent tube, in the same place in both eyes. The edge between shade and light is a clean, slightly soft wet edge.
