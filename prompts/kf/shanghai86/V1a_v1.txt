A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. One upstairs room of a lilong house in Shanghai, a summer afternoon in 1986. A three-year-old Chinese girl stands on bare floorboards facing us, left of centre, seen level with her eyes from about two metres, cut off at the hips by the bottom edge. Nearer to us, filling the right half of the frame, a lean man of about forty sits on a low wooden stool with his back three-quarters to us: we see his back, the back of his right shoulder, his upper arm coming down from the top edge, his bent elbow, his forearm, his two knees at the bottom edge and, low in the middle of the frame just above the bottom edge, his two hands held out in front of the child at the height of her tummy. His head is above the top edge of the frame and is not in the picture at all. Behind the child: a plain plastered back wall; at the far left edge a dark wooden chest of drawers; behind the man, the corner post of a wooden bed with a white mosquito net tied back to it, a straw mat on the mattress and a pile of folded cotton quilts.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She is watching his hands explain something to her and has forgotten everything else. Her arms hang at her sides. Her head is tipped a little down and perfectly still; both irises point down and slightly toward the right of the picture, at the tiny gap between his finger and thumb, fixed and bright, the way a child looks when she is counting. Her lips are just parted, her jaw loose, her brows resting. She is not smiling and not frowning and not posing for anyone: all the life in the face is in the eyes.

HIS HANDS, the second most important thing in the picture. His right hand is held up with the thumb and forefinger a hair's breadth apart, showing her something too small to see between them; the other three fingers are folded. His left hand is open beside it, palm up, its forefinger pointing at that tiny gap. He holds nothing: no ball, no dough, no object, only the small space between finger and thumb. Each hand has exactly five fingers, broad knuckles, short square nails; a cheap steel wristwatch on a worn leather strap on the left wrist; shirt cuffs rolled twice above the wrist.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. Afternoon sun comes through a window out of frame at the left and crosses the room as ONE straight diagonal shaft, from the upper left down to the lower right. Image 2 shows exactly where the sunlight falls (its white and palest shapes) and where it does not (its greys): a diagonal band across the child's chest and upper arms, the man's forearm and both his hands, and a wedge of floor and stool at the lower right. Those sunlit parts are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. Everything else, the wall, the chest of drawers, the bed, the man's back and shoulder, is in shade under one even transparent warm grey-green ink wash, a little darker in the corners, with the room's ink lines showing through it and no black anywhere. The child's face is at the top edge of the shaft and turned away from the window: on her face the wash is a thin glaze laid OVER a normally painted face: her warm rosy flesh colour and the pink blush on her cheeks still show clearly through it, never grey, never ashen, with a narrow strip of bare-paper light down the cheek on the left side of the picture. In each eye there is one small crisp catch-light of bare paper, squarish like a distant window, on the left side in both eyes. The edge between shade and sun is a clean, slightly soft wet edge. The faded red of her gingham dress is the one faint colour allowed outside the light, and it stays pale.

THE ONE THING SHE IS ATTENDING TO. The man's two hands in the light are drawn with the finest, most loving line in the picture, in a warm flesh wash, every knuckle and nail correct, with a thin margin of clean bare paper round them so they seem lit from within. Everything else stays line under wash.

LARGE round dark eyes set wide apart, each with thick dark upper lashes and a bright clear highlight; distinct, fairly strong, straight dark eyebrows; a tiny button nose; a small soft mouth; soft cheeks with a wide warm pink blush; glossy jet-black hair with a light fringe of a few soft parted strands, the forehead showing between them, and long side locks in front of the ears. Toddler proportions: a very big round head, about four heads tall, a short neck, short chubby arms and legs, small dimpled hands, a round tummy. Her face is round and short: full baby cheeks, a short chin, a small nose. Her hair is a soft bob ending at the chin, with one tiny sprout of a ponytail standing up on top of the crown, tied with a thin red thread. Clothes (1986 Shanghai, home-made, plain): a loose short-sleeved cotton smock dress to the knee in a faded pale red-and-white small gingham check, with a plain white round collar; bare arms and legs; black cotton cloth shoes with one strap, no socks. She is 3, not 5 and not a baby: she stands and sits steadily.
