SUBJECT. A three-year-old Chinese girl sits on the bare floorboards of an upstairs room in Shanghai on a summer afternoon in 1986, seen level with her eyes from just over a metre away: her head and shoulders fill the middle of the frame, cut off at the chest by the bottom edge. Scraps of torn white toilet tissue cling to her hair, her shoulder and the front of her dress. Behind her: the long side rail of a wooden bed, a straw mat on its mattress, a neat pile of three folded cotton quilts just behind her head, at the left a tall wooden bed post with a white mosquito net tied back to it, a plain plastered wall. A strip of sunlit floor along the bottom edge. Nothing else.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. The grown-ups by the door, out of frame to the right and far above her, have just burst out laughing at what she said, and she does not see what is funny. She has turned her head toward them, to the right of the picture, and lifted her chin a little to look up at them: both irises point up and to the right, at their faces, fixed and bright. There is one small puzzled crease between her strong straight brows, the inner ends of the brows pulled slightly together and up. Her lips are just parted on a short question. Nothing else in her face has moved: her cheeks are soft, her jaw loose. She is not smiling, not crying and not pouting, and not posing for anyone: she wants it explained.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. Afternoon sun comes through a window out of frame at the left; she sits in its pool of light. Image 2 shows where it falls: on her face, her neck, a diagonal patch across her left shoulder and chest (left of the picture), and the strip of floor at the bottom. The sunlit floor and the sunlit patch of her dress are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. Everything behind her, the bed, the quilts, the post, the wall, is in shade under one even transparent warm grey-green ink wash, a little darker under the bed, with the ink lines showing through it and no black anywhere. The edge between shade and sun is a clean, slightly soft wet edge. Her face is in the light: it is painted in its normal pale warm rosy flesh wash with the wide pink blush on both cheeks and no grey over it, brightest on the cheek toward the window (the left of the picture), with only the faintest warm shadow on the far cheek, under the fringe and under the chin. In each eye one small crisp catch-light of bare paper, squarish like a window, on the left side in both eyes. Her hair is glossy black ink wash with a soft sheen. The faded red of her gingham dress is the one faint colour allowed outside the light, and it stays pale.

THE TISSUE. The few scraps of torn tissue caught in her hair and on her dress are bare paper with the finest pale grey line, feathered torn edges, hair-thin fibres: small, white and exact.
