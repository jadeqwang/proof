A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. One upstairs room of a lilong house in Shanghai, a summer afternoon in 1986, seen from the eye height of a three-year-old sitting on the floor and looking steeply up, with a wide view: four adults by the door are laughing, and from down here they tower. Their legs, hands, hems and the things they hold are large and near; their heads are small, far up under the ceiling. From left to right: (1) at the far left, a grandmother sitting on the edge of a wooden bed, bent forward with laughing, in a short-sleeved cotton blouse printed with small indigo flowers on white and loose black trousers ending above the ankle, a folding paper fan half open in the hand that hangs toward us at the lower left; (2) beside her, a young aunt standing, in a pale dacron dress with a small collar and a hem just below the knee, her head thrown back, the back of one hand pressed to her mouth, the other hand hanging open; (3) in the middle, framed by the open doorway and farther away, the child's mother, smaller, standing on the threshold with one hand on the door frame; (4) at the right, a tall uncle in a white sleeveless cotton vest tucked into grey-blue twill trousers with a brown belt, leaning back with his chest and belly shaking, a white enamel mug with a lid and a dark-blue rim in one hanging hand, the other hand open and mid-slap toward his thigh. Above the two women at the left, the wooden frame of the bed's mosquito net and one diagonal bamboo pole; in the middle the plain wooden door frame with a dim landing beyond; at the right a tall dark wardrobe with a mirror door; above everything the plaster ceiling and its corner.

COMPOSITION. Follow image 1 exactly: the same camera, the same steep upward perspective, the same positions and sizes of the four figures, their hands, the door frame, the net frame, the wardrobe and the ceiling line. Image 1 is only a layout drawing from a 3D model: replace its mannequins with the real people and its boxes with the real things, and do not keep its mechanical even line. The mannequins' mitts are not hands: paint real hands with five fingers doing what is described. The slab in the lower left hand is the folding fan; put the enamel mug in the right-hand man's hand that hangs at the far right.

WHAT THEY ARE DOING. They are howling with laughter at something the child has just said, and none of it is explained to her. The laughter is all in the bodies: shoulders up, torsos shaking and tipped back or folded forward, a hand flapping, a hand on a mouth, knees giving, the mug tilting. Fine quick repeated contour lines may show a shaking shoulder, but no motion lines and no symbols.

WHO HAS A FACE. The grandmother, the aunt and the uncle have NO drawn faces. For each of them the head is a simple shape under the same wash as the wall behind it, with the hairline, ear and jaw in thin line and no features, like a figure the painter has not reached yet. Not a mask, not a white oval, not a blur. No eyes, no eyebrows, no nose, no mouth, no laughing mouth, no teeth on any of those three. Their heads are far up, tipped back, and out of the light. Only the mother's face is drawn, small and far away, in shade: she is laughing too, more quietly, eyes narrowed with it behind her round glasses, mouth open in a short laugh, looking down at the child.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The window and its sunlight are behind us; the child sits in the light but these adults stand outside it. As image 2 shows, no direct light falls on anything in this frame: there is NO bare-paper light on any figure. The whole picture is in shade under one even transparent warm grey-green ink wash, lightest low down where light bounces up off the sunlit floor onto the hems, shins, hanging hands, the fan and the mug, and deepening upward to the heads and the ceiling, darkest in the doorway behind the mother; ink lines show through it everywhere and there is no black. The nearest things, the hanging hands, the fan and the enamel mug, are drawn with the finest and most careful line in the picture, the hands in a warm flesh wash under the glaze; the higher up a figure goes the fewer and fainter the lines, until the three heads are almost nothing. On the mother's face the wash is a thin glaze laid OVER a normally painted face: warm rosy flesh showing through, never grey, never ashen.

The straight strong shoulders and upright carriage of a former competitive swimmer. Summer clothes, Shanghai 1986: a plain white short-sleeved cotton blouse with a small soft collar, loose dark trousers, black cloth shoes. No wrinkles, no sagging, no grey hair: 33.
