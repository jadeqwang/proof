A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A kitchen table in a small house in Decatur, Illinois, in 1990, in the evening, under a pendant lamp. We look straight down along a seven-year-old girl's own line of sight at her two hands on the table in front of her: her own view of them. Her two thin bare forearms come up into the picture from the bottom edge, the pushed-up cuffs of a heather-grey sweatshirt just showing at the very bottom; the hands rest near the table's edge. Her right hand, at the right of the picture, is tapping on the wood the way she plays the piano. Her left hand, at the left, lies quietly. The table is plain wood: its grain runs across the picture in long thin lines, with one old ring mark from a cup and a few small scratches. Beyond the hands, toward the top of the picture, one blank sheet of white paper lies on the table with a pencil on it. Nothing else.

COMPOSITION. Follow image 1 exactly: the same camera looking steeply down, the same positions, sizes and angles of the two forearms and the two hands. Image 1 is only a layout drawing from a 3D model: replace its mannequin arms and block hands with a real child's arms and hands, and do not keep its mechanical even line. The mannequin's hands are blocks with two or three rods for fingers: draw real hands with five fingers each, seen from above, backs of the hands toward us. The sheet of paper and the pencil are not in image 1: put them across the top of the frame, the sheet cut off by the top edge, square to the frame.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She taps when she is thinking. Her right hand is caught in the middle of a tap, in a pianist's position: the wrist level, the knuckles raised in an arch, the fingers curved. The tips of the index finger and the ring finger are down on the wood; the middle finger is lifted a centimetre above the table and about to come down; the little finger is lifted a little too; the thumb rests on its side. A small child's hand that has been trained at the piano: slim, exact, relaxed, each finger independent. Her left hand lies flat and loose, palm down, fingers a little apart, not moving. Five fingers on each hand, small nails, correct.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The lamp is directly overhead, and its pool of light covers the table: image 2 shows the whole view lit. So the table top is NOT PAINTED: it is the bare cream paper left blank, with only the thin ink lines of the wood grain, the ring mark and the outline of the sheet of paper. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. The only wash in the picture is transparent warm umber ink, used in three places: a small soft cast shadow on the wood under each hand and forearm and under each lifted finger (so we can see which fingers are in the air), a thin shadow under the pencil, and a soft gathering of the same umber in the two top corners and along the very top edge of the frame, where the table runs out of the lamp's pool. No black.

THE ONE THING IN FULL DEPTH: her hands. They are the only thing in the picture painted in full local colour and with the finest, most loving line: warm rosy flesh, a little pinker at the knuckles and fingertips, each knuckle, nail and crease drawn, the small tendons on the back of the tapping hand. Everything else is line on bare paper.

Clothes (1990 American Midwest, hand-me-down, plain): an oversized plain heather-grey crew-neck sweatshirt with the sleeves pushed up to the elbows, no print and no lettering; dark navy stirrup leggings; bare feet. Proportions of a seven-year-old: about five and a half heads tall, thin arms and legs, a big round head. She is 7: clearly older than 5 and clearly younger than 10.
