A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A kitchen table in a small house in Decatur, Illinois, in 1990, in the evening, under a pendant lamp. We look straight down along a seven-year-old girl's own line of sight at her two hands on the table in front of her. Her two thin bare forearms come up from the bottom edge, the pushed-up cuffs of a heather-grey sweatshirt at the bottom corners. Her right hand, at the right of the picture, is tapping on the wood the way she plays the piano; her left hand, at the left, lies quietly. A plain wooden table with thin grain lines, one old ring mark from a cup; beyond the hands a blank sheet of paper with a pencil lying on it.

THE ONE THING IN FULL DEPTH: her hands. They are painted in full local colour and with the finest, most loving line in the picture: warm rosy flesh, a little pinker at the knuckle dimples and fingertips, each small nail and crease drawn.
