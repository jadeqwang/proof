A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. The eat-in kitchen of a small house in Decatur, Illinois, in 1990, in the evening, under a pendant lamp that is above and out of the picture. A seven-year-old girl sits at the kitchen table facing us squarely, seen level with her eyes from close across the table: her head and shoulders in the middle of the frame, her chest, her two forearms, and her two hands on the table edge at the very bottom of the picture. Her parents are standing behind her, one at each side, talking to each other over her head; we see only their bodies from the shoulders down to the hips at the left and right edges of the frame, and their hands. At the left, her father: a pale short-sleeved shirt with a pen in the chest pocket, a dark belt, pleated trousers, his bare right forearm reaching into the picture with the hand open, palm up, stopped in the air. At the right, her mother: a soft knitted cardigan open over a collarless blouse, one hand lifted, palm turned in, fingers loose, stopped in the air too. Behind the child's head: a double-hung sash window with the night outside, its sill, a chrome tap and the edge of the sink, a laminate counter, the corner of a wall cupboard at the upper left.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. One second ago she was drumming her fingers on the table and her eyes were wandering. Her mother has just asked a question about her, in a dialect she is not supposed to understand, and she has understood it. Everything has stopped for one beat. Her hands are frozen in the middle of a tap: the right hand arched, one finger still lifted a centimetre above the wood and not coming down. Her head is perfectly still, level, facing us squarely. NOTHING in her face has changed: her lips are together and level, her jaw loose, her brows resting, exactly as before; she is not smiling, not frowning, not startled, not posing for anyone; her eyebrows are not raised and her mouth is not open. The whole event is in her eyes. They have stopped moving. Both irises are exactly parallel, not converged on anything, aimed a little below our eye line and very slightly toward the left of the picture, at empty air above the middle of the table: she is looking at nothing, because she is listening. The eyes are wide open, dark and very bright, the upper lids a hair higher than at rest so that the whole round iris shows. It is the stillness of a child who has just heard something meant for someone else, and is working it out.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The lamp is overhead, in front of her and above the frame; its pool of light falls on her and on the table. Image 2 is the lighting guide. In the light: her face, her fringe and the top of her hair, her sweatshirt, forearms and hands, the table edge, and the fronts and raised hands of the two adults at the sides. Those lit parts are NOT PAINTED with any wash: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. Her face, in the light, is fully and normally painted on that bare paper: thin fine line, her own warm rosy flesh wash, the pink blush on her cheeks, dark irises, thick upper lashes, glossy black ink hair; the softest transparent warm umber shadow under her fringe, under her chin and beside her nose, no more. In each eye one small crisp round catch-light of bare paper, high in the iris (the lamp), in the same place in both eyes. The adults' clothes in the light are line on bare paper with no colour of their own. Everything behind them is out of the light, under one even transparent warm umber ink wash: the wall, the window frame and sill, the tap, the counter, the cupboard; the night panes of the window a deeper layer of the same umber, never black; the ink lines show through. The edge between wash and blank paper is a clean, slightly soft wet edge.
