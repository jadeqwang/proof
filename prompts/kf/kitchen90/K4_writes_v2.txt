A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A kitchen table in a small house in Decatur, Illinois, in 1990, in the evening, under a pendant lamp. We look steeply down along a seven-year-old girl's own line of sight at the sheet of paper in front of her. One sheet of plain white unlined paper, completely blank. Her left hand lies flat on the left edge of the sheet to hold it still; her right hand holds an ordinary wooden pencil, its point a breath above the paper: she is about to write. Her thin bare forearms come in from the bottom corners, grey sweatshirt cuffs pushed up. At the upper right, beyond the sheet, her father's right hand rests on the table, open, palm up, fingers loosely curled.

THE ONE THING IN FULL DEPTH: her right hand and the pencil, painted with the finest, most loving line and in full local colour: warm rosy flesh, pinker at the fingertips; the pencil a plain six-sided wooden pencil painted a soft ochre gold, bare wood cone, dark graphite point, nothing printed on it. THE SHEET OF PAPER IS COMPLETELY BLANK: no writing, no letters, no lines, no ruling, no marks, no smudge.
