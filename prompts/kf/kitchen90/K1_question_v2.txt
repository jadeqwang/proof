A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. The eat-in kitchen of a small house in Decatur, Illinois, in 1990, in the evening. One pendant lamp with a cone-shaped shade over a plain wooden kitchen table is the only light. A seven-year-old girl sits at the far side of the table, facing us, small, in the lamplight, her hands on the table edge, a blank sheet of paper and a pencil in front of her. Her parents stand behind her, one on each side, talking fast to each other across the top of her head in a dialect they believe she cannot follow: her father at the left, considering; her mother at the right, in the middle of a word. Behind them a sash window over the sink with the night outside, a tap, a counter, plain wall cupboards, a refrigerator and an electric cooker at the right, the backs of two wooden chairs at the left and right edges.
