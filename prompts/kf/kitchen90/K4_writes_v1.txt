A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A kitchen table in a small house in Decatur, Illinois, in 1990, in the evening, under a pendant lamp. We look steeply down along a seven-year-old girl's own line of sight at the sheet of paper in front of her: her own view. One sheet of plain white unlined paper lies on the wooden table, completely blank. Her left hand, at the left of the picture, lies flat on the left edge of the sheet to hold it still. Her right hand, right of the middle, holds an ordinary wooden pencil, its sharpened point a few millimetres above the paper: she is about to write. Her two thin bare forearms come into the picture from the bottom corners, the pushed-up cuffs of a heather-grey sweatshirt just showing. At the right edge of the picture, beyond the sheet, a third hand rests on the table: her father's, a grown man's right hand, much larger than hers, lying open on the wood with the palm up and the fingers loosely curled, at rest, cut off at the wrist by the edge of the frame. The table is plain wood, its grain running in long thin lines.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She has heard her father's answer and is about to write it down. The pencil point hovers just above the paper, a breath from touching; the hand is poised, the wrist resting on the sheet, the fingers firm and exact, a small child's piano-trained hand. The left hand presses the sheet flat, fingers spread. Her father's hand does nothing at all: it simply lies there, open, near her. Five fingers on every hand, correct.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The lamp is directly overhead and its pool of light covers the table: image 2 shows the whole view lit. So the sheet of paper and the table top are NOT PAINTED: both are the bare cream paper left blank. The sheet is told from the table only by its thin ink outline, a hairline of shadow along its lower and right edges, and by being perfectly clean, while the table round it carries the thin ink lines of the wood grain. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. The only wash in the picture is transparent warm umber ink, used for: a small soft cast shadow under each hand and forearm and under the pencil, a soft shadow under the father's hand, and a soft gathering of the same umber in the two top corners and along the very top edge of the frame, where the table runs out of the lamp's pool. No black.

THE ONE THING IN FULL DEPTH: her right hand and the pencil. They are painted with the finest, most loving line in the picture and in full local colour: warm rosy flesh, pinker at the knuckles and fingertips, each knuckle, nail and crease drawn; the pencil a plain six-sided wooden pencil painted a soft ochre gold, with a bare wood cone and a dark graphite point, no eraser band lettering, nothing printed on it. Her left hand and her father's hand are drawn with care in thin line and a pale flesh wash. THE SHEET OF PAPER IS COMPLETELY BLANK: no writing, no letters, no lines, no ruling, no marks, no smudge.
