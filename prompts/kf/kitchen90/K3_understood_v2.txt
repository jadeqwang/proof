A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. The eat-in kitchen of a small house in Decatur, Illinois, in 1990, in the evening, under a pendant lamp that is above and out of the picture. A seven-year-old girl sits at the kitchen table facing us squarely, seen level with her eyes from close across the table, her two hands on the table edge at the bottom of the picture. Her parents stand behind her at the left and right edges, seen only from the shoulders down: her father's open palm stopped in the air at the left, her mother's lifted hand stopped in the air at the right. They are talking about her, over her head, in a dialect she is not supposed to understand. Behind her a sash window with the night outside, a tap, a counter.

COMPOSITION. Image 1 is an earlier take of this exact painting. Repaint it as it is: the same camera, the same child in the same place at the same size, the same head, hair, face shape, level closed mouth, resting brows and blush, the same sweatshirt, the same two hands frozen on the table edge, the same father's shirt, belt, trousers and open hand at the left, the same mother's cardigan, blouse and lifted hand at the right, the same window, tap, counter and cupboard, the same bare-paper lamplight on her and on the adults, the same warm umber wash behind. Image 2 is the layout drawing it was made from (a 3D model, composition only; do not keep its mechanical even line). Change only ONE thing: her eyes.

HER EYES, the whole event of the picture. In image 1 she is looking up and outward, a little above us, wide-eyed, as if at something. That is wrong. She is looking at NOTHING. One second ago her eyes were wandering after her tapping fingers; her mother has just asked a question about her, and she has understood it, and her eyes have simply stopped where they happened to be. Repaint only the eyes: both irises sit a little LOWER in the eye than in image 1 and very slightly toward the left of the picture, so that her gaze passes below us and beside us, toward empty air above the middle of the table. The two irises are exactly parallel, not converged on any point: the unfocused look of someone who is listening hard. The lids are at rest, the upper lid just touching the top of each iris; the eyes are open and dark and very bright, but not widened and not startled. Pupils large and soft. In each eye one small crisp round catch-light of bare paper, high in the iris, in the same place in both eyes. Everything else in the face stays exactly as in image 1: lips together and level, jaw loose, brows resting; she is not smiling, not frowning, not surprised, not posing for anyone. Her head stays perfectly still and level.

LIGHT. As in image 1: her face, hair, sweatshirt and hands, the table edge and the adults at the sides are in the lamplight and are NOT PAINTED with any wash: bare cream paper with thin ink outlines, her face fully and normally painted on it in warm rosy flesh with pink blush. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom. Everything behind is under one even transparent warm umber ink wash, the night panes a deeper layer of the same umber, never black.

PEOPLE. THE GIRL. Little Jade at about age 7, Decatur, Illinois, 1990: a Chinese girl in first or second grade, with LARGE round dark eyes set wide apart, each with thick dark upper lashes; distinct, fairly strong, straight dark eyebrows; a tiny button nose; a small soft mouth; a round face with soft cheeks and a wide warm pink blush, the chin a touch longer than a small child's. Glossy jet-black hair (pure black ink, never brown): a light fringe of a few soft parted strands with the forehead showing between them, long side locks in front of the ears, a small half-ponytail tied high at the back of the crown with a dark band, the rest straight down to her shoulder blades. Clothes (1990 American Midwest, hand-me-down, plain): an oversized plain heather-grey crew-neck sweatshirt with the sleeves pushed up to the elbows, no print and no lettering; dark navy stirrup leggings; bare feet. Proportions of a seven-year-old: about five and a half heads tall, thin arms and legs, a big round head. She is 7: clearly older than 5 and clearly younger than 10.
