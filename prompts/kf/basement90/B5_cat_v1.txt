A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. The unfinished basement of a house in Decatur, Illinois, in 1990, at night. Under her father's computer desk, in the dark of the knee space, a seven-year-old girl is being a cat. We are on the concrete floor at her level, looking in through the open end of the desk, straight at her face from about a metre away. She is on her hands and feet, knees tucked up under her, back rounded high like a cat's, shoulders up, chin low, her face toward us in the middle of the picture. Around her: the two square wooden legs of the desk's open end, one at the far left edge and one large and close at the right; the underside of the desk top across the top of the frame; behind her the plain side of the desk's drawer pedestal; a painted concrete-block wall at the left; a bare poured-concrete floor. Beside her at the right, past the desk leg: her father's shins in grey trousers and one brown leather lace-up shoe, as he sits at the desk above, and beyond them a spoke and a black caster of his rolling office chair.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She has gone back to pretending to be a cat, and she is perfectly content: weight settled evenly on hands and feet, as comfortable as a cat on a windowsill, in no hurry at all. Her head is perfectly still, low between her shoulders, tipped down a little so that she looks out from under her fringe. Her lips are together and level, her jaw loose, her brows resting. She is not smiling and not frowning and not posing for anyone: all the life in the face is in the eyes, which are wide, bright and fixed on one of the casters of the chair, the way a cat watches a thing that might move: both irises are turned toward the right side of the picture and a little downward.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The only light in the room is a computer screen on top of the desk, above and out of the picture; it cannot reach under the desk. So almost the whole picture is in shade, and she is entirely in it. Image 2 shows the only place the light arrives: a narrow slanting strip at the far right, on the floor beyond the desk and across her father's shin and shoe and the chair's spoke. That strip is NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. No white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. Everything else is under one transparent deep indigo ink wash (clearly blue, an ink blue, not grey), laid in several soft flat transparent layers, deepest up under the desk top and behind her, with slow cloudy variations in its depth and the paper grain and fibres still breathing through it; never flat black, never a solid fill. In that dark the desk, the wall and the floor are thin ink lines under wash with no colour of their own. On her face the wash is a thin glaze laid OVER a normally painted face: her warm rosy flesh colour and the pink blush on her cheeks still show clearly through it, never grey, never ashen; her eyes are as large as on her face sheet, with dark irises. In each eye there is one small crisp catch-light of bare paper: a short low horizontal sliver (the lit floor beyond the desk), on the right side of each iris, the same in both eyes. Her hair is glossy black ink. The edge between the dark and the strip of blank paper at the right is a clean, slightly soft wet edge.
