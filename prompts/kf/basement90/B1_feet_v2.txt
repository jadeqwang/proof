A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. The unfinished basement of a house in Decatur, Illinois, in 1990, at night. The only light anywhere is a computer screen on a desk, above and outside the picture. We are down at floor level and close: the five-spoke star base of a rolling office chair seen from the side, and standing on it the two small bare feet of a seven-year-old girl. Her father sits in the chair; she stands on its base behind him, one foot on each of two neighbouring steel spokes, to keep her feet off the cold concrete floor. We see her only from the shins down: thin legs in dark navy stirrup leggings (the strap of each legging passes under the arch of the foot; heel and toes are bare). Behind, all in shade: the chair's gas column and the underside of its seat; her father's two leather lace-up shoes and trouser cuffs at the left; one wooden desk leg at the far left edge; a painted concrete-block wall and the desk's drawer pedestal; a stack of blank fan-fold printer paper on the floor at the right. A bare poured-concrete floor with one hairline crack.

COMPOSITION. Image 1 is an earlier take of this exact painting. Repaint it as it is: the same camera, the same chair base, spokes and casters, the same two legs and two feet in the same places at the same size, the same toes curled over the spokes and the same lifted heels, the same father's shoes and trousers, desk leg, wall, drawers and paper stack. Image 2 is the layout drawing it was made from (a 3D model, composition only; do not keep its mechanical even line). Change only these four things.
ONE, the painting hand. Image 1 is modelled too heavily, like a tonal pencil rendering. Repaint it in the film's hand: thin, even, delicate ink outlines and FLAT transparent wash, with almost no modelling: no soft shading round the forms, no highlights on the metal, no leather texture, no fabric texture. Shapes are told by line; tone is told by one wash.
TWO, the shade. Everything the screen light does not reach is under ONE even transparent wash of deep indigo (a blue-violet ink blue, clearly blue, not grey), soft and a little deeper under the desk and the seat; never flat black. Under it nothing has a colour of its own: the father's shoes are NOT brown and his trousers are NOT grey: they are ink line under the same indigo wash as the wall behind them.
THREE, the light. Where the screen light falls, the paper is left completely blank, the same bare cream as the border of an unpainted sheet, with only thin outlines: the wedge of concrete floor across the right foreground, the paper stack, the top faces of the two spokes she stands on. No grey tone there at all, no white paint, no glow, no rays. The boundary on the floor is one straight, clean, slightly soft wet edge, running from the lower left toward the middle right as in image 1.
FOUR, her feet are a seven-year-old's: a little smaller than in image 1, shorter and rounder, with short round toes and a soft instep, not long and elegant.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She is balancing, a little precariously. Her weight is on the balls of her feet; the toes of each foot are curled down over the edge of its spoke to hold on; both heels are lifted and hang in the air a few centimetres above the concrete. Five toes on each foot, small and correct.

THE ONE THING SHE IS ATTENDING TO, and the centre of the picture: her two bare feet. They are the only thing painted in full local colour, with the finest, most loving line in the picture: warm rosy flesh on bare paper, pinker at the toes and the balls of the feet because the floor is cold, every toe and small nail drawn, the ankle bone, the dark stirrup strap passing under each arch. Leave a thin margin of clean bare paper round the feet so that they seem lit from within. Everything else in the frame stays line under wash.

Glossy jet-black hair (pure black ink, never brown): a light fringe of a few soft parted strands with the forehead showing between them, long side locks in front of the ears, a small half-ponytail tied high at the back of the crown with a dark band, the rest straight down to her shoulder blades. Clothes (1990 American Midwest, hand-me-down, plain): an oversized plain heather-grey crew-neck sweatshirt with the sleeves pushed up to the elbows, no print and no lettering; dark navy stirrup leggings; bare feet. Proportions of a seven-year-old: about five and a half heads tall, thin arms and legs, a big round head. She is 7: clearly older than 5 and clearly younger than 10.
