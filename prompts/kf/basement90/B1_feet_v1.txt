A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. The unfinished basement of a house in Decatur, Illinois, in 1990, at night. The only light anywhere is a computer screen on a desk, above and outside the picture. We are down at floor level and close: the five-spoke star base of a rolling office chair seen from the side, and standing on it the two small bare feet of a seven-year-old girl. Her father sits in the chair; she stands on its base behind him, one foot on each of two neighbouring steel spokes, to keep her feet off the cold concrete floor. We see her only from the shins down: thin legs in dark navy stirrup leggings (the strap of each legging passes under the arch of the foot; heel and toes are bare). Around and behind the feet, all in shade: the chair's steel gas column rising from the hub to the underside of the padded seat at the top of the frame; the five slim pressed-steel spokes, each ending in a black twin-wheel caster; her father's two brown leather lace-up shoes flat on the floor beyond the chair base at the left, and the cuffs of his grey trousers above them; at the far left edge one square wooden leg of the desk, with the underside of the desktop above it; behind, a painted concrete-block wall and the side of the desk's drawer pedestal; on the floor at the right, a stack of blank fan-fold printer paper with perforated tractor-feed edges. The floor is a bare poured-concrete slab, smooth and cold, with one hairline crack.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She is balancing, a little precariously. Her weight is on the balls of her feet; the toes of each foot are curled down over the edge of its spoke to hold on; both heels are lifted and hang in the air a few centimetres above the concrete, so that under each heel there is a clear gap and then the floor. The tendon at the back of each ankle shows. The foot nearer to us, at the right, is turned a little outward; the other foot is on the next spoke, further back and to the left. Five toes on each foot, small and correct.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The light is the glow of the computer screen, above and out of frame at the upper left, coming down over the front edge of the desk. Image 2 shows where it falls (its lighter shapes) and where it does not (its dark greys): it falls on her two feet and the fronts of her shins, on the top faces of the two spokes she stands on, on a wedge of the concrete floor in the right foreground, and on the paper stack at the right. Those lit parts are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. No white paint, no blue glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. Everything else, the wall, the desk, the father's legs and shoes, the seat, the gas column, the other three spokes, the casters and most of the floor, is under one even transparent deep indigo ink wash, laid in soft transparent layers, a little deeper under the desk and under the seat, with the ink lines showing through it and the paper grain breathing through it; soft, never flat black, never a solid fill. Nothing under the wash has any colour of its own. The edge between wash and blank paper is a clean, slightly soft wet edge; on the floor it is one straight soft line, the shadow of the desk's front edge.

THE ONE THING SHE IS ATTENDING TO, and the centre of the picture: her two bare feet. They are the only thing painted in full local colour, with the finest, most loving line in the picture: warm rosy flesh on bare paper, pinker at the toes and the balls of the feet because the floor is cold, every toe and small nail drawn, the arch, the ankle bone, the tendon, the dark stirrup strap passing under each arch. On the side of each foot that faces the screen, the faintest transparent glaze of pale screen blue. Leave a thin margin of clean bare paper round the feet so that they seem lit from within.
