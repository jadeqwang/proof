A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. The produce aisle of a plain neighbourhood supermarket in Skokie, Illinois, 1993, under rows of bare fluorescent tubes. We are low down among the produce on an island bin, just behind ONE green globe artichoke that lies on its heap at the bottom of the frame, and we look up the aisle from there. In the middle distance a mother is walking her ten-year-old daughter away from us up the aisle, holding the girl by the right forearm; the girl goes with her but has turned her head right back over her shoulder to keep looking at the artichoke. On the right, nearer to us, an elderly woman in a wool coat and headscarf, seen from behind, pushes her small wire shopping cart past along the grocery shelving: she has finally got through. Left foreground: the mounded produce of the bin, big round heads of cabbage and grapefruit, a heap of oranges, and at the upper left a pale spiky romanesco cauliflower sitting on the heap. Right: a long grocery shelving unit running away up the aisle, packed with plain boxes, jars and tins. Above: a white drop ceiling of square acoustic tiles and chain-hung strips of two bare fluorescent tubes. Bare smooth concrete floor. No awnings, no banners, no signs.

COMPOSITION. Follow image 1 exactly: the same low camera, the same perspective, the same positions and sizes of the three people, the cart, the shelving, the heaps and the tube strips. In image 1: the small figure in the centre with a face is the GIRL; the taller figure just right of her, back to us, is her MOTHER; the large figure at the right, back to us, hands forward on a bar, is the OLD WOMAN, and the slab and wheels in front of her are her wire shopping cart; the big balls at the left and bottom are heads of cabbage, grapefruit and oranges; the spiky mass at the upper left is the romanesco; the cluster of leaf shapes at the bottom centre, cut off by the bottom edge, is THE ARTICHOKE, seen from just behind and above; the long slabs at the right are shelves of groceries. Mannequin hands are mitts: draw real hands.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She is being walked away and is still counting. Her body faces away from us up the aisle, in mid-step beside her mother, her long hair down her back; her right forearm is held in her mother's left hand and drawn forward. Her head is turned as far back over her shoulder as it will go, so that we see her face in three-quarter view: both irises are fixed on the artichoke at the bottom of the frame, just below us, bright and exact, the way a child looks when she is counting and has not finished. Her lips are level and barely apart, as if the next number is still coming; her jaw is loose, her brows resting. She is not smiling and not frowning and not posing for anyone: all the life in the face is in the eyes. Her free left hand hangs open at her side.
The mother does not look back: she walks on, upright, her left hand closed firmly round the girl's forearm, the grip of someone moving a child quickly out of other people's way, firm and sure, with care in it.
The old woman takes small quick steps, a little stooped, both hands on the handle bar of her cart, already level with the two of them and passing on the right.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. Image 2 shows the light: rows of bare tubes overhead, so the light is even and there is almost no shadow anywhere. The fluorescent tubes themselves are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. Everything else, ceiling, shelves, floor, cart, the three people, every heap of produce and the romanesco, is under one even transparent pale flat GREY ink wash: a cool neutral grey like diluted ink, clearly greyer and a step darker than the bare cream paper of the tubes, never beige, never sepia, the same value everywhere, with the ink lines showing through it and NO local colour: the produce is thin grey line under the pale grey wash, not green, not orange, not purple, not dark. No black anywhere, no cast shadows. On the girl's face the wash is a thin glaze laid OVER a normally painted face: her warm rosy flesh colour and the pink blush on her cheek still show clearly through it, never grey, never ashen; in each eye one small crisp catch-light of bare paper, a short thin horizontal bar like a distant fluorescent tube. Her hair and her mother's hair are soft black ink. Clothes keep only the faintest trace of their colour under the grey.

THE ONE THING SHE IS ATTENDING TO. The artichoke at the bottom centre is the only object in the picture painted in full local colour and full depth: a real globe artichoke lying on the heap, sage and grey-green, each overlapping bract drawn separately with the finest, most loving line in the picture, each with a small notch at its tip and a violet-tinged edge. Round it leave a thin margin of clean bare cream paper, NOT PAINTED, so that it seems lit from within. The other artichokes in the heap beside it, and the romanesco, are only grey line under the wash.

She is 10: a child, about as tall as her mother's shoulder.
THE MOTHER, seen from behind (image 5 is her model sheet: use its back view for her build and her hair only). A Chinese woman of forty with the straight strong shoulders and upright carriage of a former swimmer; glossy jet-black hair in a soft perm of loose springy curls ending at the jaw, never longer. Here she wears a plain knee-length wool winter coat, buttoned, and dark trousers and flat shoes. We do not see her face.
THE OLD WOMAN, seen from behind (image 6 is her costume sheet: use its back view, its hands and its cart). Small, rounded, about seventy-five, a slight forward stoop. A knee-length nubbly bouclé wool car coat a little too big for her, a thin headscarf with small dots covering her head and tied under the chin, a straight tweed skirt below the coat, thick stockings a little wrinkled at the ankle, pale lace-up walking shoes with thick soles, a boxy handbag hanging from the crook of her left elbow, a small upright wire shopping cart with a paper grocery bag in it. Coat, scarf, hands and shoes are drawn with care in thin line, under the same grey wash as the shelves. Her face is turned away from us and is not drawn: only the back of the headscarf and a little of the cheek line, no features, like a figure the painter has not reached yet. Not a mask, not a white oval, not a blur.
