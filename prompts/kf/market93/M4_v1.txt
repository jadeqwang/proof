A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. The produce aisle of a plain neighbourhood supermarket in Skokie, Illinois, 1993. A close view, looking down from in front and a little above, at the middle of a ten-year-old girl who stands holding ONE green globe artichoke in both hands at her right side of the frame. Her mother's hand has just closed on the girl's bare right forearm to move her out of the way. We see no faces: the frame holds the girl's body from chest to thighs, her two bare forearms and hands, the artichoke, and the mother's coat sleeve and hand coming in from the upper left. At the far left edge, a part of the mother's plain winter coat. Along the right edge, the slanted dark side of a low produce bin with a few round fruit in it. Below and behind, a bare smooth concrete floor.

WHAT IS HAPPENING. The mother's right hand has come down onto the girl's right forearm, halfway between wrist and elbow, and closed round it: four fingers wrapped over the top of the forearm, the thumb round the near side, the whole palm in contact. It is the grip of someone moving a child quickly out of other people's way: firm, sure and fast, with care in it; the hand of a mother who has done this a hundred times. Not a pinch, not a claw, no white knuckles, no nails digging in; the child's skin gives only slightly under the fingers. The forearm has just begun to be drawn toward the left. The girl has not let go: both her hands are still cupped round the artichoke, fingers spread to keep it level, exactly as before, as if the arm belonged to someone else. Every hand has five fingers, carefully drawn.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. Image 2 shows the light: even light from rows of fluorescent tubes overhead, no direction and almost no shadow. Everything except the artichoke is under one even transparent pale flat GREY ink wash: a cool neutral grey like diluted ink, never beige, never sepia, the same value everywhere, with the ink lines showing through it and no local colour: the coat, the shirt, the trousers, the bin and the floor are thin line under the pale grey wash. On the hands and forearms the wash is a thin glaze laid OVER normally painted skin: their warm rosy flesh colour still shows clearly through it, never grey, never ashen. No black, no cast shadows, no glow, no rays, no haze.

THE ONE THING SHE IS ATTENDING TO. The artichoke in her hands is the only object in the picture painted in full local colour and full depth: a real globe artichoke, sage and grey-green, each overlapping bract drawn separately with the finest, most loving line in the picture, each with a small notch at its tip and a violet-tinged edge, the bracts spiralling out from its closed tip, which points up toward where her face would be. Round the artichoke leave a thin margin of clean bare cream paper, NOT PAINTED, so that it seems lit from within. Nothing else in the frame has colour of its own except the skin of the hands.
