A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A naturalist's study, seen through the eyes of a ten-year-old girl in a supermarket in 1993: she is looking down, from close, onto ONE head of romanesco (romanesco broccoli, the lime-green fractal cauliflower) lying in a produce bin. We look down onto its crown, a little from the front: a dome of pointed cone-shaped florets spiralling out from the small tight tip, every floret a pointed little pyramid that is itself built of a spiral of smaller pointed florets, and those of smaller ones again. It fills the centre of the frame. Round it, cropped by the edges of the frame, the big rounded shapes of the other heads in the bin: plain cabbages and cauliflowers wrapped in their leaves. Nothing else: no hands, no people, no price cards.

COMPOSITION. Follow image 1 exactly: the same view from above, the same size and position of the romanesco in the centre of the frame, the tiny tip of the spiral a little above the middle of the head, and above all the SAME ARRANGEMENT OF FLORETS: one pointed floret for each shape in image 1, in the same places, overlapping the same way, the smallest crowded round the tip and each one a little larger than the last as they wind outward, the largest standing out round the rim as separate points. Image 1 is a construction drawing of the true arrangement of this plant (each floret 137.5 degrees round from the last, which makes thirteen spiral arms sweeping one way and eight the other): do not add florets, do not remove any, do not rearrange them into rings or rows. Replace its mechanical even line with a living drawn line, and turn each plain cone into a real romanesco floret. The large rounded outlines at the edges of image 1 are the neighbouring cabbages and cauliflowers in the bin.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The even light of rows of fluorescent tubes overhead: no direction, almost no shadow. Everything except the romanesco, the neighbouring heads, their leaves, the bin, is under one even transparent pale flat GREY ink wash: a cool neutral grey like diluted ink, never beige, never sepia, the same value everywhere, the ink lines showing through it and NO local colour: thin grey line under pale grey wash, not green. No black, no cast shadows, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom.

THE ONE THING SHE IS ATTENDING TO. The romanesco is the only object in the picture painted in full local colour and full depth, with the finest, most loving line in the picture, the way a botanical painter would do it, floret by floret. Its colour is a clear pale chartreuse, lime green going to creamy yellow-green at the points and a cooler green in the crevices between florets. Every floret is a firm pointed cone, a small copy of the whole head: its surface is studded with its own spiral of tiny pointed buds, large at its base and shrinking to its tip, and on the bigger florets each of those buds is visibly a cone of still smaller grains. Spiral within spiral within spiral, down to the limit of the brush; the finest work is at the centre, where the florets become too small to count. The florets run out from the tip in curving arms, thirteen one way and eight the other, exactly as laid out in image 1, clear enough that a child could follow one arm with her finger from the tip to the rim. Modelling is by transparent colour: a slightly deeper green on the shaded side of each cone, no dark shadow. A few small blue-green leaves cup the base where it shows at the rim. Round the whole romanesco leave a thin margin of clean bare cream paper, NOT PAINTED, between it and the grey wash, so that it seems lit from within. Everything else in the frame stays line under wash with no colour of its own.
