A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. A naturalist's study, seen through the eyes of a ten-year-old girl in a supermarket in 1993: she is looking straight down at ONE green globe artichoke that she holds in front of her chest, its tip pointing up at her, so that we look down onto its crown and see the whole rosette of overlapping bracts spiralling out from the closed centre. It fills the centre of the frame. Her own two hands are the only other things in the picture: her left hand cups the artichoke from underneath on the left, and the tip of her right index finger touches one bract. Behind and below, far out of focus, only a bare smooth concrete floor. Nothing else: no table, no other vegetables, no people.

COMPOSITION. Follow image 1 exactly: the same view straight down onto the crown, the same size and position of the artichoke in the centre of the frame, and above all the SAME ARRANGEMENT OF BRACTS, one bract for each shape in image 1, in the same places, overlapping the same way, the tiny closed centre a little below and right of the middle of the artichoke. Image 1 is a construction drawing of the true leaf arrangement of this plant (each leaf 137.5 degrees round from the last): do not add bracts, do not remove any, do not rearrange them into rings. Replace its mechanical even line with a living drawn line, and give each plain shape the real form of an artichoke bract. The two slanting lines at the lower left and lower right of image 1 are her bare forearms coming in from the two bottom corners; the hands themselves are not in image 1 and are described below.

HER HANDS. The hands of a ten-year-old child, small, with short clean nails, bare forearms (shirt sleeves rolled up out of frame), each hand with five fingers, drawn in thin line. LEFT HAND (lower left): it holds the artichoke from below, palm up under its base, the four fingertips curling up into view round the left rim of the artichoke and the thumb resting against the near rim; the artichoke hides the palm. RIGHT HAND (lower right): it comes in from the lower right corner, loosely closed, with the index finger alone extended; the pad of that fingertip rests lightly on the edge of one outer bract on the right side of the artichoke, at about four o'clock. She is counting, and the finger marks her place. The finger does not cover the centre of the artichoke.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. The light is the even light of rows of fluorescent tubes: no direction, almost no shadow. The floor behind and the hands are under one even transparent pale flat grey ink wash, cool and very light, the same value everywhere, with no local colour: on the hands the wash is a thin glaze laid over normally painted skin, so their warm rosy flesh colour still shows through faintly, never ashen. No black, no cast shadows, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom.

THE ONE THING SHE IS ATTENDING TO. The artichoke is the only object in the picture painted in full local colour and full depth, with the finest, most loving line in the picture, the way a botanical painter would do it, bract by bract. Every bract is a thick fleshy scale, broad and rounded, cupped round the head, with a shallow NOTCH in the middle of its tip and a tiny dry point in the notch, fine parallel veins fanning from its base, a cool sage and grey-green face that is paler and yellower toward the base where the next bract covers it, and a VIOLET-tinged edge and tip, strongest on the outer bracts. The bracts at the centre are small, tight and paler, closed over the tip like a bud; they grow steadily larger toward the outside. Each bract sits in the gap between the two below it, so the bracts run out from the centre in curving spiral arms: eight arms sweeping one way and five the other, exactly as laid out in image 1. Each bract casts only the thinnest soft tone on the one beneath it; modelling is by transparent colour, not by dark shadow. Round the whole artichoke leave a thin margin of clean bare cream paper, NOT PAINTED, between it and the grey wash, so that it seems lit from within. Everything else in the frame stays line under wash with no colour of its own.
