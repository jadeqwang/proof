A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. The produce aisle of a plain neighbourhood supermarket in Skokie, Illinois, in 1993, under rows of bare fluorescent tubes. A ten-year-old Chinese girl stands still in the middle of the aisle, facing us, seen level with her eyes from a metre and a half away, cut off below the waist by the bottom edge. She holds ONE green globe artichoke in both hands in front of her at the bottom of the frame, and she is looking down at it. Directly behind her, half hidden by her, an elderly woman in a wool coat and headscarf waits with both hands on the handle of a small wire shopping cart: she cannot get past. On the left a long grocery shelving unit runs away from us: its lower shelves hold plain jars, tins and boxes, and its two top shelves, level with her head, hold only a few large plain cartons with wide blank sides, and in front of it at the lower left a pallet stacked with big plain cardboard melon cartons that narrows the aisle. On the right the corner of a low produce island bin with mounded heaps of produce: round cabbages, eggplants, grapefruit, a heap of artichokes, and at the right edge two pale spiky romanesco cauliflowers; a few small blank price cards on thin stems stand in the heaps. At the back a refrigerated wall case with shelves of produce and the plain back wall; far back by that wall two small shoppers in coats cross the aisle with their backs to us. Above: a white drop ceiling of square acoustic tiles and chain-hung strips of two bare fluorescent tubes running away in rows. Bare smooth concrete floor. No awnings, no banners, no signs.

The girl's head is just left of the centre of the frame, in the upper half; her hands and the artichoke are at the bottom centre. Image 1 is only a layout drawing from a 3D model: replace its mannequins with the real people and its boxes, slabs and balls with the real things named above (the big balls at the lower right are cabbages and grapefruit, the long slabs at the left are shelves of groceries, the boxes at the lower left are cardboard cartons, the box behind the girl is the wire cart), and do not keep its mechanical even line. Keep the area at the upper left, beside her head, plain and quiet: wide blank carton sides and bare shelf edge, a calm empty area with very few lines, and keep the ceiling at the upper right plain too.

WHAT SHE IS DOING. She is counting the spirals of leaves on the artichoke. She holds it in both hands, fingers cupped under and round it, its stem toward us and its tip tilted up toward her face. Her head is perfectly still and tipped a few degrees down; both irises are lowered and converged on the tip of the artichoke in her hands, so that we see her eyes under their upper lids and lashes. Her lips are level and barely apart, as if a number is about to be said under her breath; her jaw is loose, her brows resting. She is not smiling and not frowning and not posing for anyone: all the life in the face is in the eyes, which are fixed and bright on the artichoke, the way a child looks when she is counting. She has no idea anyone is behind her. Each hand has five fingers.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. Image 2 shows the light: rows of bare tubes overhead, so the light is even and there is almost no shadow anywhere. The fluorescent tubes themselves are NOT PAINTED: bare cream paper left blank, with only thin ink outlines. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. Everything else in the room, ceiling, shelves, cartons, floor, cart, the old woman, the heaps of produce, is under one even transparent pale flat GREY ink wash: a cool neutral grey like diluted ink, clearly greyer and a step darker than the bare cream paper of the tubes, never beige, never sepia, never brown, the same value everywhere, with the ink lines showing through it and NO local colour at all: every heap of produce, including the other artichokes, the eggplants and the cabbages, is thin grey line under the pale grey wash, not green, not purple, not orange, not dark. No black anywhere, no cast shadows. On the girl the wash is a thin glaze laid OVER a normally painted face: her warm rosy flesh colour and the pink blush on her cheeks still show clearly through it, never grey, never ashen; her hair is soft black ink; her shirt and trousers keep only a faint trace of their colour under the grey. In each eye there is one small crisp catch-light of bare paper, a short thin horizontal bar like a distant fluorescent tube, on the same side in both eyes.

THE ONE THING SHE IS ATTENDING TO. The artichoke in her hands is the only object in the picture painted in full local colour and full depth: a real globe artichoke, grey-green and sage, each overlapping bract drawn separately with the finest, most loving line in the picture, each with a small notch at its tip and a violet-tinged edge, a short cut stem pointing toward us. Round the artichoke leave a thin margin of clean bare paper, so that it seems lit from within. Nothing else in the frame has colour of its own except her face and hands.

Little Jade at about age 10, Skokie, Illinois, 1993: a Chinese girl in fourth or fifth grade. LARGE round dark eyes set wide apart, each with thick dark upper lashes and a bright clear highlight; distinct, fairly strong, straight dark eyebrows; a tiny button nose; a small soft mouth; soft cheeks with a wide warm pink blush; a soft oval face with a small soft chin. Glossy jet-black hair, long and straight down to the middle of her back: a small half-ponytail tied high at the back of the crown with a dark band, long side locks in front of the ears, the fringe grown out into a few long thin strands parted to both sides so that most of the forehead shows. A loose pale-blue chambray button shirt worn open like a jacket over a plain white T-shirt, sleeves rolled up to the elbows so the forearms are bare; straight khaki-tan cotton trousers. No print, no lettering, no jewellery, no glasses. She is 10: a child, not a teenager.
THE OLD WOMAN BEHIND HER (image 5 is her costume sheet). Small, rounded, about seventy-five, a slight forward stoop, patient. A knee-length nubbly bouclé wool car coat with large round buttons, a thin headscarf with small dots tied under the chin, both hands folded on the handle bar of a small upright wire shopping cart with a paper grocery bag in it. She is entirely under the same pale grey wash as the shelves behind her, with no colour of her own: coat, scarf and hands in careful thin line under the wash, easy to miss. Her face is not drawn: the head is a simple shape under the same wash as the wall behind it, with the edge of the headscarf, a little hair and the jaw in thin line and no features, like a figure the painter has not reached yet. Not a mask, not a white oval, not a blur.
