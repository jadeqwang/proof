A painting on cream xuan paper, wide 16:9, in one hand: fine-line baimiao ink outlines and transparent ink wash, the paper grain and a few long paper fibres showing through everywhere.

SUBJECT. An attic room above a restaurant in the Netherlands, a winter morning in 1988. Bare wide floorboards, a sloping roof with exposed rafters, a low knee wall, and ONE small square window of four panes set low in the gable wall at the right. Along the far wall, thin sleeping mats with folded quilts and pillows; under the window a leaning stack of old textbooks; at the far left a small 1980s television on a low cabinet. In the lower left a five-year-old Chinese girl sits cross-legged on the floorboards, seen from behind and a little above, her back to us, leaning forward with both small hands on a paper printout of piano keys that lies flat on the floor in front of her: two sheets of ordinary printer paper taped end to end, black and white piano keys printed on them, soft map-fold creases across the paper.

LIGHT, the most important thing in the picture. Low winter sun comes through the four panes and crosses the room as ONE straight diagonal shaft, from the window at the upper right down to the floor at the lower left, ending on the girl's lap and the paper keys. Image 2 shows exactly where the sunlight falls (its white shapes) and where it does not (its greys). The shaft of light, the lit patch of floor, the lit paper keys and the window panes are NOT PAINTED AT ALL: they are the bare cream paper left blank, with only the thinnest ink outlines inside them. No yellow, no white paint, no glow, no rays, no haze, no bloom: light is simply where the brush did not go. Everything the sun does not touch is under one even transparent cool blue-grey ink wash, a little darker under the roof and in the far corners, with the room's ink lines showing through it and no other colour in it. Where the shaft crosses the air in front of the far wall and the mats, the wash stops at the shaft's two straight edges and the paper is bare between them, so the shaft reads as a clean band of blank paper lying diagonally across the painted room. The edge between wash and blank paper is a clean, slightly soft wet edge. On each window pane, a pale frost fern drawn in the faintest grey line.

Glossy jet-black hair, a small half-ponytail tied high at the back of the crown, the rest straight to just past her shoulders. Her head, shoulders and back are above the light and under the blue-grey wash; her forearms, hands, knees and the paper keys are in the light, and those hands and keys are the one place in the picture drawn with the finest line and full care.
