## 2. The reading, revised

- The blank face is a space left blank. Everyone fills it in with a guess: cute, anxious, dissociating, oblivious, perky.
- Her two triumphs are both proofreading. She catches the word Dad's find-and-replace missed, and she catches the misaligned row on the handout. So "proof" has a fifth meaning, and it is the one the scenes actually stage: the proof copy, the page that gets marked up.
- She proofreads the world while the world proofreads her face.
- Her attention is the evidence of an inside. "I am paying attention to the—" is the line nobody lets her finish.
- The people who love her read her hands, not her face.
- Three experiments rhyme across 37 years: toilet paper torn to atoms, a toilet flushed under five conditions, a party reviewed as telemetry.

## 3. Vision: "Stet"

*Stet* is the proofreader's mark for "let it stand": ignore the correction, leave it as it was.

**The video is a marked-up proof copy.**

- **Paper is the material.** Toilet paper, Dad's application papers, the yellow handout, the red dictionary, the bookshelf, a word processor. Each memory is a printed page from its own era, which fits the house habit of one art style per section:

| Memory | Print style |
|---|---|
| Shanghai 1986 | 连环画 picture-story-book line drawing |
| Decatur 1990 | WordPerfect blue screen, text-mode characters |
| Elementary school | Photocopied worksheet, speckled, copy of a copy, clip-art people |
| Skokie 1993 | Supermarket-circular halftone |
| Housewarming | Over-bright glossy photograph, 15% too saturated |

- **Attention is rendering.** The world stays flat print. Whatever she is attending to is the only thing drawn in full luminous depth: the toilet-paper fibres, the one missed word on the blue screen, the misaligned row, the artichoke. Her face is blank and the frame shows exactly where her mind is. (This inverts *Rewind*, where she was the only thing in colour.)
- **Two sets of marks, drawn by the renderer.** Red correction marks are other people's edits to her: a caret inserting a smile, "more expression", a circle round her face. Gold pencil marks are her catches on the world: the missed word, the short row, the golden angle on the artichoke. Palette from the Spaces Left Blank site: warm paper, graphite, gold, plus correction red and WordPerfect blue.
- **The shelf is the hub.** Each verse is a volume pulled from her bookshelf. The bridge reveals the shelf itself: beauty on this side, truth on this side, science fiction among the truth. One set, modelled in three.js, that every section returns to.
- **The ending is one word.** Her resting face, covered in everyone's red corrections. A gold pencil writes *stet* in the margin and dots the line under her face. The red marks stay visible, overruled. That replaces the ∎.

**Wordless sections are still the interiors**, now in her native formats:

## 10. Alternative organising ideas

1. **The unfinished sentence.** "I am paying attention to the—". Every scene is her attention at full depth, then an interruption: laughter, an arm pulled, a question, a correction. The outro is the first time nobody interrupts, and the thought finishes. "I can think again" becomes literal.
2. **Field notes.** The book's own subtitle: "field notes on... human experience from the inside". She is the naturalist and the humans are the specimens. Only her gold pencil annotates the frame. The chorus is the one observation she can't make, because she is inside the apparatus.
3. **Me-in-translation.** The little red dictionary, "we don't have a word for this", English from books. The subtitle track becomes expressive: her taps get subtitles, the chorus gets an empty bracket, the outro needs none.
4. **留白 (leaving blank).** In Chinese painting the unpainted paper is the mist, the water, the sky: the part that holds the most. Other people's readings of her are painted on in ink; what is hers stays as luminous paper. Answers "flat affect" in the culture's own terms and is the project's title.
5. **Instrument flight.** From "Calibration Telemetry" and the artist commentary: with no horizon to look at, she flies by instruments. One instrument per scene. Coldest of the five and closest to the machine reading.

Recommendation: 1 as the spine, 3's subtitled taps as a device, 4 as the look principle.

## 11. Direction chosen 2026-11-22: field notes + 留白 + the kid who finds patterns

Jade's picks: **field notes** as the organising principle (it is Book 2's theme), **留白** as the look principle, and the pattern-finding child as the thing to explore. Her images: dust motes in a sunbeam when she looks up from a book by the attic window; a mote in the beam of light she can see from inside the wall; Earth in the Voyager photo as a mote of dust in a sunbeam; fractals in the attic window and in a romanesco; the artichoke, annotated in her mind, the spiral count interrupted.

### How they lock together

- **The beam is 留白.** A sunbeam on xuan paper is drawn by not painting it: dark wash around a band of bare paper, motes as flecks. Light is paper. Same for the blade of light in the wall and the band of scattered light in the Voyager frame.
- **One composition, three times.** Attic beam, wall beam, Voyager beam: a diagonal band of bare paper with one speck in it. A triple match cut.
- **The mote in the wall answers the chorus.** "How do you feel? Inside?" can cut to inside the wall: dark, one blade of light, a child's eye following one mote. "I honestly don't know": she was watching the dust. It shows attention, not fear, so it reads without the poems and means more with them. This settles the raid question at the readers-only level without staging the raid.
- **Scale is the pattern she finds.** Tissue torn ever tinier, a mote, the Earth as a mote. The piano break can go in through a tissue fibre and come out at the pale blue dot.
- **Dendrites.** The frost on the attic window is dendritic. So are the neurons she grows up to study. The pattern the five-year-old traces on the glass is the shape of the thing doing the tracing. That is a picture of "inside" for the second vocalise.

### Field notes as a visual language

- Gold pencil hairline, drawn by the renderer, one annotation per shot.
- The hand ages with her. At three: dots and finger-traces, no numerals. At seven: tallies. At ten: numbered spirals. As an adult: hug durations, pitch contours, room-crossing velocity. Verse 3's review is the same notebook turned on people.
- "Did she understand or just pattern match?" gets answered by the film: pattern-finding is how she understands, and it is beautiful.
- Notes on humans are mostly "?" at first. Over the laughing adults in verse 1: a single gold question mark.
- **The interrupted count.** In the supermarket, gold numerals land on the artichoke's spiral arms as she counts. The arm is pulled before the last one. The count resumes and completes in the arpeggio interlude, one numeral per note, while she is being walked away. Tearing tissue gives a second sequence: 1, 2, 4, 8.
- **The one thing never annotated is her own face.** The notebook page for "How do you feel? Inside?" stays blank. After a film of gold notes on everything else, the last shot is her face in the beam with no mark on it. 留白: the blank is the part that holds the most. That replaces "stet".

## 12. The wall (notes 2026-11-22)

From Jade: the wall was in a different part of the building, so it is its own set. Her mother came, grabbed her and made her run, snapping her out of deep thought. She suggests a cross-section like the Leonov airlock cutaway in Orbital Sunrise (`1-music-shipped/orbital-sunrise-video/media/plates/airlock_cut/still.jpg`: a side cutaway floating on black, the body filling the tube), because otherwise it is hard to show what it was like, unless it is kept very simple.

### What the grab adds

- It is another interruption of deep thought, and the first one in her life with stakes.
- It rhymes with the supermarket: "She pulls my arm and physically moves me out of the lane." Same hand, same forearm, same framing in both shots. The viewer learns why this mother pulls, without a word about it.
- It gives the mother's "you need to pay attention" and "so they don't know" their history.

### Two shots, and the second one is the dial

1. **Simple (always in).** Dark frame, one diagonal blade of bare paper, one fleck, one eye catching the light. No architecture. With the lyric "Inside?" it reads as inside something; readers of the poems know what. It matches the attic beam and the Voyager frame exactly.
2. **Cutaway (the slot).** Pull back to a side section of the wall: two faces, studs, a gap barely wider than her shoulders, the child in it, the crack of light. Drawn as a field-note diagram, the way a naturalist draws an animal in its burrow. Cavity in dark wash, beam as bare paper, one gold ring round the mote. The room outside the wall is empty line drawing.

Shot 1 alone is the readers-only level. Shots 1 and 2 make it plain she is hidden in a wall. Anything shown outside the wall would make it explicit. So the undecided question becomes one shot in or out, with no re-timing.

Every other scene carries a place-and-year header. This one has none.

### Devon Market reference (gathered 2026-11-22)

Seven photos and `SOURCES.md` in `inputs/refs_devon_market/`; `01_interior_produce_island_and_aisle_2025.jpg` is the best single one.

Seen in the photos:
- White drop ceiling of square acoustic tiles, long continuous rows of bare tube lights, white round columns.
- Loose fruit heaped in mounds on low dark slant-sided island bins, in colour blocks.
- Timber posts carrying green striped canvas awnings over the islands; tubes under the canopies.
- Hanging steel pan scales, plastic-bag roll stands, cartons and crates stacked at island ends.
- Printed red-and-white oval price cards on stems stuck into the piles.
- Aisles about two carts wide, shelving packed full, cartons on the floor.

Leave out for 1993 (the reference shop itself only opened in 2001): the wood-look plank floor (plain vinyl tile is a guess for the period), LED-look tubes, security cameras, clamshell tubs and bagged-salad walls, stickers on every fruit, barcode shelf tags, lowercase aisle signs.

Use for the film:

- Mounded produce in colour blocks is easy to hold flat in halftone, with one artichoke and one romanesco in full depth.
- A hanging pan scale is a period instrument already in the room: a ready-made field-note prop.
